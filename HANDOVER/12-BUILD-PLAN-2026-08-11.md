# 0 · THE SPINE — AKIF'S FRAMEWORK, RESTATED

Everything below is arranged on the five pillars the SECP Chairman gave on
23 July, in his order and his vocabulary. When this becomes a presentation, this
is the table of contents, because it answers him in the language he used.

| Pillar | His instruction | Where it lives in this plan |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Onboarding** | Keep barriers low. Filter, do not enforce. Show a member only what they are eligible for. | §3 Mechanism · §5 Open vs Known · §11 Consumers and savers |
| **2 · Collection** | "Does auto-debit *truly* work?" Raast is getting proper auto-debit. Build the collection standard around it. | §2 Collection · §6 HYPER |
| **3 · Pooling products** | Never hold the money. Link to a licensed online AMC and a trustee — CDC. Revive the vault through them. | §7 Vault · §12 Institutions |
| **4 · Prevention** | Build so there is **no incentive to default**, or consequences so great that nobody does. Cover defaults on Halqa's own balance sheet, and insure that exposure with takaful. | §1 Exit · §4 Buy turns · §8 Ownership · §10 The cover facility |
| **5 · Recovery** | Find a *real* recovery way. | §8 Ownership · §10 Set-off · §3.6 What we do instead of publishing addresses |

His two structural instructions, which cut across all five:

1. **Separate consumers from savers.** A consumer takes the pot early and spends
   it; enforce PG and stronger terms. A saver waits, and is routed to the vault
   and the AMC. Halqa has been building one product for both. §11 splits them.
2. **Filtering over enforcement.** The affordability engine and the seat bands
   are not user-facing rules, they are an invisible filter on what Discover
   shows. A member should never see a circle they cannot afford or a seat they
   cannot hold.

**The single derived principle in this document.** Every risk control and every
price below is a function of one quantity — the **forward liability**:

```
L(k) = c × (n − k)          contribution c, size n, seat k
```

It is what the member owes the group at the moment they collect. It is the only
thing at risk anywhere in the model. Section 5.3 shows that the chairman's own
rule — *"unblock mid turns after position 5 if the committee is out of 12"* —
falls straight out of a forward-liability ceiling of seven contributions, and
therefore generalises to every circle size without another arbitrary number.

# 1 · CANCELLATION AND EXIT

## 1.1 · What the chairman specified, and what it costs

> *"If cancel before pot, remaining money installment reimbursed at end with
> fines. If insured committee, then pot not affected by this person."*

This sits on top of the five-level exit ladder already ruled on 9 August. The two
rules combine cleanly:

| Level | Trigger | Fine | Restitution | Target share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Window** | Inside the 24h `CONFIRMING` window | none | full, immediate | — |
| **2 · Substitution** | A replacement takes the seat | none | replacement pays the leaver their paid-to-date **directly** | **~90% of all exits** |
| **3 · Group-approved** | Majority vote of the circle | **1 installment** | paid-to-date, **at cycle end** | small |
| **4 · Hardship** | Recorded statement to the helpline | **waived** | paid-to-date, at cycle end | rare |
| **5 · Abandonment** | Stops paying, no request | full recovery machinery | withheld and set off | must be rare |

Level 2 is the one to engineer for. A substituted exit costs the circle nothing,
costs Halqa nothing, and is settled the same day. **Every exit screen must offer
substitution first and make it one tap** — "find me a replacement" — before the
member is ever shown the vote path.

## 1.2 · The restitution arithmetic (uninsured circle)

A member exits after paying `p` installments and before collecting. The circle
contracts from `n` to `n−1` seats, so every remaining pot falls from `n·c` to
`(n−1)·c`. The `p` members who already collected the full `n·c` are each ahead by
exactly `c`.

**Therefore each of those `p` members owes the leaver exactly `c`, settled at
completion. Total `p·c`, nominal, no time value.**

Worked: 12 members × Rs 10,000, exit after round 4.

```
Leaver has paid                                   4 × 10,000 = Rs 40,000
Rounds 1–4 collectors each received                          Rs 120,000
Had the leaver never joined they would have received         Rs 110,000
Each therefore owes                                           Rs 10,000
Four of them                                                  Rs 40,000
Leaver receives at cycle close, less fine                     Rs 40,000 − 10,000 = Rs 30,000
```

Ledger invariant at close: for every continuing member `received − paid = 0`;
every exited member is returned to zero less their fine. The rule is recursive —
a second exit re-runs the same arithmetic against the already-adjusted roster.

**The fine is the entire deterrent.** No time value on the returned money, and
one installment gone. A member who wants out in month two of a twelve-month
circle loses one installment and waits ten months for the rest. That is severe
enough without a cancel button, and mild enough that it is not predatory.

**Fine destination: 70% to the remaining members** (their pots shrank; they are
the injured party), **30% to Halqa** as administration. Halqa must never be the
main beneficiary of an exit.

## 1.3 · The insured circle — "pot not affected by this person"

With cover on (`Committee.coverEnabled`), the circle **does not contract**. The
cover facility buys the vacated seat, pays `c` every remaining round, and
collects that seat's pot at its scheduled turn.

Consequences, all of them good:

- Every other member's pot stays `n·c`. Nobody is diluted. This is precisely the
  chairman's requirement.
- **No restitution obligations are created between members.** The party that
  gained is the cover facility, so the facility returns the leaver's `p·c` at
  cycle close, less the fine.
- The leaver's exit becomes an operational event rather than a group negotiation,
  which is why insured (open) circles can run **Level 3 without a vote** — the
  group has nothing to lose, so there is nothing to vote on. Uninsured (known)
  circles keep the vote, because the group genuinely bears the loss.

That single difference — vote or no vote — is the cleanest way to explain to a
member why open circles carry a cover premium and known circles do not.

## 1.4 · Pay-on-behalf — the "Sponsor"

> *"Can request someone to pay on behalf either at premium or free. You pay them
> only in consecutive pot on their behalf for their turn, or auto-deduct from
> your pot."*

This is the best feature in the brief. It is what real committees already do
informally, it converts a would-be default into a member-funded save, and it is a
natural viral loop — a sponsor is usually someone not yet on the platform.

Three repayment modes, all built, one default:

| Mode | Mechanism | Premium | Shariah |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reciprocal** (default) | The sponsor pays your round `k`; you pay their round `k+1` (or a later named round). A swap of obligations, no money lent. | **0** | Clean — a mutual favour, *qard hasan* in substance |
| **Pot deduction** | The sponsor pays your round; the amount is netted from your pot at your turn and paid to them. | 0 or negotiated | Clean at 0. Priced = a fee on a cash advance. |
| **Cash** | Settled between the two of them off-platform; Halqa records the substitution only. | outside | outside |

**Premium rules.** Cap at **5% of the installment**, Halqa takes **10% of the
premium** (the same rate as the turn marketplace). On a Rs 20,000 installment
that is a Rs 1,000 maximum premium and Rs 100 to Halqa.

**Label it honestly.** A priced sponsorship is a fee on a loan. It stays in the
product under the standing rule — never remove a feature for being non-Shariah —
but it is never labelled Shariah, it is never the default, and the free
reciprocal option is always shown first and shown larger. On Shariah-labelled
circles only the zero-premium modes are offered, exactly as the marketplace
already works.

**Guard rails.** A member may hold at most **2 open sponsorships**; a sponsor may
carry at most **5 concurrent sponsorships** across the platform; sponsorship of a
member with an open recovery case is blocked; a sponsored payment counts as
**on-time for the circle and neutral for Sakh** — it does not earn a streak, and
it does not damage the score. Sponsorship must never become a way to buy a clean
record.

## 1.5 · Payout deduction order — exact

Everything above converges on one function. When a pot is released, deductions
apply in this order, and the total is capped at **60% of the pot** (the same cap
as the existing forward-liability holdback, for the same reason — a member's turn
must never be emptied):

```
 1. Own unpaid contribution for the current round      (already blocks release)
 2. Own current-round installment, netted              (§2.6 — default ON)
 3. Sponsor repayments due, oldest first
 4. Restitution obligations owed to exited members that have fallen due
 5. Cover premium arrears and late fees
 6. Forward-liability security shortfall                (existing, ≤60%)
                                              ─────────────────────────
                                              TOTAL CAP  60% of pot
```

Anything above the cap rolls forward to the next deduction event and is disclosed
to the member on the payout screen in rupees, before release.

# 2 · COLLECTION

## 2.1 · The calendar

The chairman's cycle is the 9th and the 10th. Layered onto the payday collection
already shipped:

| When | Event | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| Payday eve | Balance-check notice, WhatsApp + push | `SHIPPED` |
| **Payday morning** | Mandate executes while the balance is at its monthly maximum, *before* the installment is due. Government salaries land on the **1st**, often earlier before Eid; private payrolls run the 1st–7th. | `SHIPPED` |
| Payday → 9th | Retry ladder, Raast one-tap request, WhatsApp nudge on each failure | `SHIPPED` |
| Payday → 9th | Money already collected sits with the **trustee**, in the payer's own name, earning | `STAGE-2` |
| **9th, 23:59 PKT** | Final sweep. Anything unpaid enters the late ladder. | `BUILD` |
| **10th, 09:00 PKT** | Single payout release for every circle on the standard calendar | `BUILD` |

The due date already stopped being the collection event. It is the deadline for
*failures*. The 9th/10th pair simply makes the settlement day uniform across the
platform, which is what makes a single daily trustee sweep possible at all.

## 2.2 · The float is small. Say so.

```
Pot Rs 120,000 · mean hold 6 days · Islamic money-market yield 10.5% p.a.
120,000 × 0.105 × 6/365                                    = Rs 207 per round
Over a 12-round cycle                                      = Rs 2,486 per circle
Halqa's 10% share                                          = Rs 249 per circle-cycle
```

**Rs 249 per circle per year is not a revenue line.** It is a member benefit and
a marketing line — *"your committee money earns while it waits, which it never
did before"* — and it should be presented as exactly that and nothing more. The
real AUM revenue is the Growth Vault distributor trail in §7.5, which is two
orders of magnitude larger.

The engineering requirement the float creates is real even though the money is
small: **no payout may ever wait on a fund redemption.** The 10th is funded from
the AMC's settlement account or the cover facility; the redemption settles behind
it. This is a hard invariant, tested.

## 2.3 · Rails and their true cost

Cost of collecting one **Rs 20,000** installment:

| Rail | Member pays | Halqa pays | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Raast RTP one-tap** (push) | Rs 0 | ≈ Rs 0 | **Default for everyone, today** |
| **Raast direct debit** (pull) | Rs 0 | ≈ Rs 0 | **The target.** SBP scheme in testing; realistically 9–15 months |
| Intra-wallet (JazzCash→JazzCash) | Rs 0 | Rs 0 | Use whenever both sides are on the same wallet |
| Wallet pull under mandate | Rs 0 | **Rs 300–600** | Fallback only |
| Card | Rs 0 | **Rs 693** (3.3% + Rs 33) | **Not offered on standard circles** |
| Cash via agent | Rs 0–50 | Rs 20–60 | Last resort; it destroys the digital record, which is the asset |

**The binding constraint.** Members pay Rs 0, so every rupee of rail cost is
unrecovered, and the chairman's ceiling is Rs 300 per payment. A 3% wallet pull
breaches that ceiling above a **Rs 10,000** installment. Therefore:

```
installment ≤ Rs 10,000   →  wallet pull permitted as fallback
installment > Rs 10,000   →  Raast only; wallet pull requires a negotiated flat fee
```

A twelve-member circle running a full cycle on card rails would burn **Rs 52,272**
in fees against zero member revenue. Raast at ≈0% is the only arithmetic under
which free-for-members survives. This is why §6 makes Raast a hard gate on HYPER.

**On cheques.** The cheque was removed from the model as a *security* instrument
when the §489-F criminal route was abandoned, and it stays removed. As a
*collection* rail it is not viable either — physical deposit, one to three days'
clearing, and dishonour risk sitting on the wrong side of the payout date. The
free universal rail is Raast RTP, and that is the one to push.

## 2.4 · Chat and phone numbers

`ChatMessage` and `ChatRead` already exist. What is missing is the policy, and
the policy matters more than the code.

| Circle type | In-app chat | Phone numbers |
|---|---|---|
| **Known / Apna** | Group thread, on by default | **Visible to members** once the 24h window closes — they already know each other |
| **Open** | Group thread, on by default | **Never shown.** A "request contact" both parties must accept; the host sees a masked number and escalates to Halqa |
| **HYPER** | **No chat at all** | Never — the product is anonymous by design (§6) |

Handing eleven strangers each other's phone numbers is the exact mechanism that
produced the harassment complaints behind the 400 banned lending apps. Halqa does
not need to send a single message to be blamed for what happens on a number it
published. The cost of the restriction is near zero; the cost of getting it wrong
is the company.

Chat is also where the recovery evidence lives, so: messages retained for the
cycle plus 24 months, exportable into the evidence pack, and a report button that
files a `SecurityEvent`.

## 2.5 · Streaks, points and rewards

| Event | Points | Sakh |
|---|---|---|
| On-time installment | **+10** | +2, capped at +40 per cycle |
| Paid 3+ days early | **+15** | +2 |
| Clean round while on a streak | ×(1 + 0.05 per consecutive clean round, **cap +50%**) | — |
| Circle completed clean | **+200** | existing completion event |
| Sponsored someone's installment | **+50** | 0 |
| Missed | streak resets to 0 | existing ladder |

The +50% streak cap reuses the constant already in the group-staking code, so
there is one number, not two.

**Points are never redeemable for cash.** Cash redemption converts points into a
stored-value balance, which is e-money, which is the EMI licence. Redemption menu:

- Early-seat fee waiver (25,000 pts)
- Slot fee waiver (10,000 pts)
- One month's cover premium (5,000 pts)
- Marketplace commission waiver on one accepted bid (15,000 pts)
- Waitlist priority token — jumps the **queue**, never the **seat order** (3,000 pts)
- Merchant vouchers, where the merchant funds them (variable)

Points and Sakh stay separable: points buy discounts, Sakh buys seats. Never let
points buy score, and never sell score.

## 2.6 · Netting the collector's own installment

> *"On your pot turn, option to deduct that month's installment from the pot,
> auto."*

Make it the **default and non-optional**. `CommitteeMember.deductOwnInstallment`
defaults true and the toggle is not exposed.

**Reason 1 — it removes a pointless round-trip.** The member would pay `c` and
receive `n·c` on the same morning. Net payout is `(n−1)·c`. Unconditional, true
on every rail.

**Reason 2 — it breaks a deadlock, and this is the real argument.** The existing
rule is that a round's payout cannot release while *any* contribution in that
round is unpaid, **including the recipient's own**. So today:

```
09:00  the mandate pulls c from the recipient's account
       the account is short — this is the exact morning they are shortest,
       because the pot has not landed yet
       the pull FAILS
       their own unpaid contribution now BLOCKS their own payout
       the payout is the money that would have funded the pull
```

That is circular. The member is denied the pot because they could not pay an
installment that the pot itself pays. Netting deletes this specific case
entirely, and it is a live production state today, not a theoretical one.

Precisely: netting does **not** remove the whole "payout blocked by an unpaid
contribution" class — another member missing still blocks the round, and it
should. It removes exactly one sub-case: **the recipient blocking their own
payout.** That was overstated in the first draft and this is the correct claim.

**Reason 3 — a rail saving that is real but conditional, and smaller than first
stated.** Pulls per full cycle:

```
without netting        n²      = 144   (12-member circle)
with netting           n² − n  = 132
avoided                n       =  12   pulls, 8.3% of all pulls
```

The rupee value depends **entirely on the rail mix**, and the first draft quoted
the 100%-wallet case as though it were the number:

| Rail mix | Wallet pulls avoided | Saved per circle-cycle |
|---|---|---|
| 100% Raast (the target) | 0 | **Rs 0** |
| 20% wallet fallback | 2.4 | **Rs 720–1,440** |
| 50% wallet fallback | 6 | Rs 1,800–3,600 |
| 100% wallet | 12 | Rs 3,600–7,200 |

So the honest statement is: **on Raast the rail saving is zero and the case for
netting rests entirely on reasons 1 and 2.** The saving is a fallback-mix bonus
that shrinks as the collection thesis succeeds, which is the correct direction
for it to move. Do not put the Rs 3,600–7,200 figure in a deck without the rail
mix next to it.

Presented to the member as: *"You don't pay in the month you collect — it's
already taken out."* One sentence, and it is the truth.

## 2.7 · Raast — putting it in place

### 2.7.1 · What Raast is, and how a company like ours reaches it

Raast is SBP's own instant payment system. Its **participants are regulated
entities** — banks, microfinance banks, EMIs and PSO/PSPs. **A fintech does not
join Raast directly.** Halqa reaches it as a **merchant of a licensed payment
aggregator** (PayFast, Safepay) or through a PSO. That is a commercial merchant
agreement with an already-licensed entity, which is exactly the Gate-2 item
already budgeted, and it does not create a bank partnership.

Schemes, and which ones we use:

| Scheme | What it does | Our use |
|---|---|---|
| **Raast ID** | Mobile number resolves to an IBAN and the registered account title | **Identity + the account-title check** already used in salary verification |
| **P2P credit transfer** | Instant push, free by SBP mandate | **Payouts** |
| **Raast QR / P2M** | Merchant collection | Not our shape |
| **Request-to-Pay (RTP)** | A payee requests; the payer approves in their own bank app; funds move instantly | **The collection workhorse today** |
| **Direct debit / recurring mandate** | Silent pull under a standing mandate | **The target. Status unconfirmed — verify with SBP and the aggregator before anything is planned around it.** |

### 2.7.2 · The one question that decides the architecture

This is the most important open item in the whole Gate-2 conversation, and it
costs one email to resolve:

```
Can Halqa initiate a Raast Request-to-Pay in which the PAYEE is another
member's Raast ID, rather than Halqa's own merchant account?

YES →  True member-to-member. Halqa is never in the funds path. The record-only
       architecture holds exactly as it is documented and as the code already
       assumes. Build (A).

NO  →  Funds land in the AGGREGATOR's settlement account and Halqa instructs a
       payout to the recipient. The licensed aggregator holds; Halqa has
       instruction rights and no beneficial ownership. Build (B) — and the
       counsel opinion must confirm in writing that this is not custody by
       Halqa, because the entire licensing position rests on it.
```

**Ask PayFast, Safepay and 1LINK this question in writing, before signing
anything.** The ledger already models member→member, so answer (A) requires no
change and answer (B) requires a settlement-account entity in the ledger and a
revised legal opinion.

### 2.7.3 · The collection ladder, stated honestly

| Rung | Mechanism | Silent? | Cost per Rs 20,000 | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wallet or card mandate | **Yes** | Rs 300–693 | Works today. Breaches the Rs 300 ceiling above a Rs 10,000 installment. |
| 2 | **Raast RTP one-tap** | **No — the member taps approve** | ≈ Rs 0 | **Works today. This is the workhorse.** |
| 3 | **Raast direct debit** | Yes | ≈ Rs 0 | **The target. Not confirmed shipped.** |
| 4 | Agent cash | No | Rs 20–60 | Last resort, and it destroys the digital record |

**On Raast today, "auto-debit" is a notification the member taps — not a silent
pull.** Silent pull exists only on wallets and cards, and those are the expensive
rails. The payday flagship still works exactly as designed, because the tap goes
out on payday morning when the balance is at its monthly maximum, but it is a
tap. Product copy must not promise otherwise, and neither should the deck.

### 2.7.4 · The build

Implement `RaastProvider` behind the existing interface in
`src/lib/payment-provider.ts`, alongside the sandbox provider, keeping the 501
soft-fail as the unconfigured path. Nothing above the provider layer changes.

```
resolveAlias(raastId)          → { iban, accountTitle }   powers the title check
createRTP({ payerAlias, payeeAlias, amountPaisa, reference, expiresAt })
getRTPStatus(rtpId)
credit({ payeeAlias, amountPaisa, reference })            payouts
POST /webhooks/raast                                       signature-verified
```

- **Idempotency.** Every RTP carries the `PaymentAttempt.id` as its end-to-end
  reference. The webhook settles against that reference inside the existing
  transaction, so a duplicate webhook is a no-op. This is the pattern already in
  use; do not invent a second one.
- **Reconciliation, non-negotiable.** A daily settlement file from the aggregator
  matched line-by-line against `LedgerEntry`, with any mismatch raising an alert.
  Without reconciliation a "settlement" is still an assertion, which is the one
  thing the whole company exists to stop being.
- **Distinct failure semantics**, because they drive different ladders:
  `EXPIRED` → resend the request · `DECLINED` → escalate to WhatsApp and offer a
  second rail · `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` → re-attempt on the next detected credit
  alert, which is what the salary-signal engine is for.
- **Rollout:** aggregator sandbox → a Rs 10 live transfer between two accounts we
  control → one real circle → general availability.

### 2.7.5 · What to get in writing in the merchant agreement

The free-for-members model is a contractual position, not a technical one. Get
these in the signed document, not in an email:

1. **The Raast MDR schedule**, including whether P2P/RTP is zero-rated to us and
   what happens if SBP changes it.
2. **Third-party payee support** — the §2.7.2 answer, in writing.
3. **Settlement timing** — funds available to the recipient the same day, since
   the 10th is a fixed payout date.
4. **Webhook delivery guarantees and replay behaviour.**
5. **A per-transaction cap** and a monthly volume commitment we can actually meet.
6. **Refund and reversal mechanics**, because a reversed contribution after a
   payout has released is the ugliest state in the system.

# 3 · MECHANISM

## 3.1 · Join authentication `BUILD` / `PARTNER`

PIN on every open (shipped) + device biometric + **NADRA face match**. Procured
through the **Nishan Pakistan portal**; the contactless Multi-Biometric
Verification Service was built at SBP's request for banking and payments.

Face match runs **once at onboarding** and re-triggers on: a new device, a
contribution above threshold, claiming a first-half seat, **any exit or hardship
request**, and 90-day dormancy. Exit is on that list deliberately — an
impersonated exit is the cheapest attack on the restitution engine.

Cost discipline: the NADRA facial certificate is reported at **Rs 20**;
Easypaisa charges **Rs 99 for a check that fails**. Do not face-check trivial
actions. **Never store raw templates or images — only the result and the NADRA
reference ID.**

## 3.2 · The 24-hour window `BUILD`

New `CommitteeStatus` value `CONFIRMING` between `FORMING` and `ACTIVE`. Host
presses Start → the roster locks against new joins → 24 hours in which any member
withdraws free, silently, with no record. Agreements are signed in this window.
Everyone sees the final roster, the seat order, and **their own total forward
obligation in rupees**. The window runs from Start, not from each join, so the
whole circle reconsiders together. Withdrawals below the minimum drop it back to
`FORMING`.

Precedent to cite: SBP already mandates a two-hour cooling period on
branchless-banking cash-outs (April 2023). We are giving twelve times that on a
twelve-month commitment.

## 3.3 · Time value of money — optional everywhere

The early-turn pricing feature stays, and per the chairman it is **optional in
every circle type**. `Committee.timeValueEnabled`, host-set at creation, forced
off on Shariah-labelled tiers, off by default on Apna circles, on by default on
open circles. When it is off, seat 1 and seat 12 cost the same and the order is
purely a scheduling choice.

## 3.4 · The invisible filter

Three caps, evaluated live at join and at host, **tightest binds**:

```
Cash flow      Σ contributions ≤ 33% of net monthly income
               Σ contributions + known debt service ≤ 40%   (TASDEEQ where available)
Forward        Σ L(k) across all circles ≤ 4 × net monthly income
Concurrency    unverified member → 1 circle · verified → 4
               unproven host → 2 circles · 5 clean → manual review
```

The 40% figure anchors to SBP's consumer-financing DBR cap (BPRD Circular Letter
29 of 2021, cut from 50%). We sit stricter at 33% on contributions alone. The
concurrency ceiling on hosts is also the anti-Ponzi control.

Evidence tiers: declared-only → one small circle. Payslip **or** a salary pattern
proven from our own collection outcomes (free, no document required) → full caps.
Extra income → the member contacts Halqa with proof, and an analyst uplift is
filed against a named reviewer with an expiry date.

**The discipline that must not be broken:** the progressive-lending literature is
unambiguous that escalating limits cause liquidity defaults when the limit
outruns real capacity. So **good history unlocks seats, circles and friction — it
never raises the money cap. Only new income evidence does.**

And never show a bare rejection. Always show headroom in rupees: *"You can add
Rs 4,000 a month. Circles up to Rs 4,000 are shown."*

## 3.5 · Order setting — three modes, one of them new

| Mode | Mechanism | Shariah | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pick-your-seat** | Slot map shows only band-eligible and tenure-eligible free seats. A claimed seat is permanent. | Clean | `SHIPPED` |
| **Verifiable ballot** | Commit–reveal draw, below | Clean | `BUILD` |
| **Formation seat auction** | Members bid a premium once, at formation, for early seats; premiums distribute to the members who take later seats; Halqa takes 10% | **Not Shariah** — it prices time | `BUILD` |

**The verifiable ballot, exactly.** At formation the server publishes
`H = SHA-256(serverSeed)`. During the 24-hour window each member submits a
`clientSeed`. At the draw the server reveals `serverSeed`; the order is
`sort by HMAC-SHA256(serverSeed, memberId ‖ clientSeed₁ ‖ … ‖ clientSeedₙ)`.
Anyone can recompute it from the receipt. This costs a day of work and
permanently kills *"the host rigged the parchi"*, which is the single most common
complaint about ballot committees in the field research.

**On the auction, be precise about which auction.** The **per-round discount
auction** — the Indian chit fund, where the highest discount bid takes the pot —
stays **REFUSED**. A worked example gives a 45–55% implied APR; it is interest in
substance and it is fatal to the positioning. What the chairman described is
different: a **one-time seat auction at formation**, which is economically the
same instrument as the turn marketplace we already run, just executed once
up-front rather than peer-to-peer mid-cycle. Under the standing rule it gets
built, it never carries a Shariah label, and the free alternatives sit next to it.

## 3.6 · Publishing a defaulter's home address — no

> *"If someone defaults, then their live house location put in committee open."*

This one I am not going to build, and I want the reasoning on record rather than
a quiet omission.

1. It is the **nano-lending playbook**. Contact-list shaming and address exposure
   are precisely what got roughly 400 apps removed and drove the SECP action.
   Google's Personal Loans policy bans the underlying data use outright, and it
   bans it **even with user consent** — consent does not cure it.
2. It fails the **journalist threshold**, which for a trust company sits well
   below the legal one. *"App publishes debtors' home addresses to strangers"* is
   a finished headline. There is no version of Halqa that survives it.
3. It creates a **physical safety exposure** we would have manufactured. Eleven
   strangers, one address, one angry evening.
4. The address has real value in exactly one place — **service of legal notice**
   — and publishing it to the group does not improve that value at all.

**What achieves the same objective, legally and better:**

- **Service-of-notice consent at signup.** The member agrees that legal notice may
  be served at the pinned address. Recovery under Order XXXVII CPC then never
  stalls on "we could not find them", which is the actual failure mode.
- **The evidence pack.** Signed undertaking, mutual PG, CNIC linkage, GPS pin,
  timestamped payment history, chat log, e-signature hashes — assembled on demand
  and handed to counsel or the court. The address goes there, not to the group.
- **Status, not address, to the circle.** *"Recovery case open · notice served ·
  Sakh −200 · platform lock."* That is the consequence members actually care
  about, and it is defensible in front of any regulator.
- **Address-cluster analysis stays internal** — it is a strong fraud signal, and
  it is invisible-and-defensive, which is the permitted usage class.

The chairman's underlying instinct is right: the consequence must be *real* and
must be *felt*. The evidence pack plus a served notice plus a −200 Sakh plus a
platform-wide lock is a harder consequence than a published address, and it is
one we can say out loud.

# 4 · BUYING TURNS

## 4.1 · What exists `SHIPPED`

`ExchangeListing` / `ExchangeBid`. Premium capped at **50% of payout**, each bid
must beat the floor, **Halqa takes 10% of the accepted premium**,
Shariah-labelled tiers permit zero-premium swaps only, the Rebuilding band is

## 4.2 · What the chairman added

## 4.3 · The swap validity rule — symmetric, both parties, both seats

A swap is **two joins happening at once**. The mistake to avoid is checking only
the buyer. Both members are landing on a seat neither of them was cleared for at
join, so **both must be re-evaluated from scratch against their new seat, using
exactly the same gate a fresh joiner would face.**

The engine therefore does not have a "buyer check" and a "seller check". It has
**one function, run twice**:

```
seatEligible(member M, seat s, circle C) :=
    1.  M.band allows seat s                      §5.3  L(s) ≤ band multiple × c
    2.  M.tenure allows seat s                    new members: last-3 only, NEVER waived
    3.  M has affordability headroom for L(s)     across ALL their circles, §3.4
    4.  M meets the seat's verification bar        first-half seats: salary/income verified
    5.  M has no open recovery case, no linked open default, not BANNED/REMOVED
    6.  M's Sakh is re-read live at execution, not at listing

swapAllowed(B at seat i, S at seat j) :=
        seatEligible(B, j, C)          the buyer moving to the seller's seat
    AND seatEligible(S, i, C)          THE SELLER MOVING TO THE BUYER'S SEAT
    AND circleLevel(C)
```

`circleLevel(C)`: the circle is ACTIVE; neither seat `i` nor seat `j` has paid
out; round `min(i,j)` is **at least 3 days away** so mandates and the ledger
re-point cleanly; and the swap does not push the circle's count of unverified
members in the first half above its cap.

**Why the seller side is not automatic.** The instinct is that the seller always
moves *later*, so they are always safe. That is only true in one of the two flows:

| Flow | Buyer goes | Seller goes | Seller's check |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Listing** — a member lists an early seat and a later member buys it | earlier | **later** | Band and tenure trivially pass; **affordability and verification still run** |
| **Bid** — a member bids on a specific seat inside a live circle (the new control) | either direction | **either direction** | **Full check. The seller can land in a seat they were never eligible for.** |

The bid control you asked for makes the second flow the common one. A member at
seat 3 who bids for seat 1 pushes the seat-1 holder **into seat 3** — still the
first half, still a restricted seat, and that member may sit below the band for
it, may be inside tenure quarantine, or may not have the income headroom for the
extra `2c` of forward liability. **That swap must be refused even though both
people want it, and even though money has already been agreed.**

Worked refusal, so the rule is unambiguous:

```
Circle: 12 members × Rs 10,000.  Band rule §5.3: Decent may hold seat ≥ 5.
B  seat 9, Sakh 760 (Excellent), 4 circles completed   — bids Rs 8,000 for seat 3
S  seat 3, Sakh 610 (Decent), 2 circles completed      — accepts

seatEligible(B, 3)  →  band ok (Excellent), tenure ok, headroom for L(3)=Rs 90,000 ok  ✔
seatEligible(S, 9)  →  band: Decent needs k ≥ 5.  Seat 9 ≥ 5.                          ✔
                       affordability: L(9) = Rs 30,000 < L(3) = Rs 90,000, exposure FALLS ✔
RESULT: allowed.

Same circle, reverse the bid:
B  seat 3, Sakh 760   — bids for seat 1
S  seat 1, Sakh 610 (Decent)
seatEligible(B, 1)  →  ✔
seatEligible(S, 3)  →  band: Decent needs k ≥ 5.  Seat 3 < 5.                          ✘
RESULT: REFUSED, and the refusal names the reason to both parties:
        "This swap would put the other member in a seat their Sakh does not allow."
```

**Two rules never relax, in any circle type, for any price:**

- **Tenure quarantine** (check 2). A new member takes a last-three seat. It is
  not for sale, not to them and not to a counterparty.
- **Affordability** (check 3). It protects the member from the group, not the
  group from the member, so consent does not waive it.

The known-circle relaxation reaches checks 1 and 4 only, and it reaches them
**for both parties equally** — there is no version where the buyer is checked
loosely and the seller strictly, or the reverse.

**Consent, both sides.** Because the seller is being moved, the accept step is a
second explicit consent showing the seller, in rupees: their new seat, their new
collection date, their new forward liability `L(i)`, and the premium they
receive. `ExchangeBid` gains `sellerSeatAfter`, `sellerLiabilityAfterPaisa` and
`sellerConsentAt`, and the accept endpoint re-runs `seatEligible` on **both**
members at execution time — not at listing time, because a Sakh can move or a
recovery case can open between the two.

## 4.4 · The circle credit rating

A buyer bidding into someone else's circle is buying that circle's payment
behaviour. Show it, as a grade and as two numbers:

```
CircleGrade = 0.40 × normalised mean Sakh of the roster
            + 0.30 × on-time payment ratio to date
            + 0.15 × verified-income share of the roster
            + 0.10 × host record (circles completed clean, defaults incurred)
            + 0.05 × authenticity and concentration flags
```

Rendered **A / B / C / D**, with a floor rule: any open recovery case in the
circle caps the grade at C; two caps it at D.

Alongside the grade, the two numbers that actually determine the buyer's risk:

- **Rounds remaining**, and
- **Residual forward liability of the circle** — `Σ L(k)` over members who have
  not yet collected — shown in rupees.

And one line of disclosure that must not be softened: **buying an early seat buys
the obligation attached to it.** Seat 1 of a twelve-member Rs 10,000 circle is
Rs 120,000 now and Rs 110,000 owed. That sentence goes on the bid screen.

## 5.2 · Where the fee numbers come from

The cover premium is not a guess. Expected loss per member-cycle is
`q × mean L(k) = q × c(n−1)/2`, where `q` is post-payout default probability.
Premium collected per member-cycle at rate `r` on each installment is `r × c × n`.
Setting a **3× loading** for adverse selection, operating cost and takaful
reinsurance:

```
r × c × n = 3 × q × c (n−1)/2
       r  = 1.5 q (n−1)/n  ≈  1.5 q
```

**Premium rate ≈ 1.5 × the default rate, independent of circle size.**

```
Open circles      q ≈ 1.0%   →  r ≈ 1.5%    ✔ matches the table
Known circles     q ≈ 0.3%   →  r ≈ 0.5%    ✔
HYPER             q ≈ 2.0%   →  r ≈ 3.0%    ✔ §6
```

The consequence, stated plainly: **every price in this document is a function of
a modelled `q` in the 0.3–1.4% band, and the first hundred completed circles
reprice all of them.** That is the honest way to present the fee book to Akif —
not as numbers we invented, but as one formula and one empirical input we have
not measured yet.

Sanity check on the ceiling: 1.5% of a Rs 20,000 installment is exactly Rs 300,
which sits on the chairman's ceiling. So the premium carries a hard rupee cap of
**Rs 300** and tapers in percentage terms above a Rs 20,000 installment.

## 5.3 · The unlock ladder — and where "position 5 of 12" comes from

> *"For decent, you unblock mid turns after position 5 if the committee is out of
> 12 — find such a ratio for smaller and larger committees."*

Do not fit a ratio. Fit the **forward-liability ceiling**, and the seat rule falls
out on its own:

```
Rebuilding (<550)      L(k) ≤ 2c     ⇒  k ≥ n − 2     the last 3 seats
Decent     (550–649)   L(k) ≤ 7c     ⇒  k ≥ n − 7
Good       (650–749)   any seat
Excellent  (750+)      any seat
```

Check it against his own number: `n = 12`, Decent → `k ≥ 5`. **Position 5 of 12,
exactly as he specified**, derived rather than assumed. And it generalises with no
further tuning:

|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | seats 4–6 | **any seat** (max exposure 5c < 7c) | any |
| 10 | seats 8–10 | seats 3–10 | any |
| **12** | seats 10–12 | **seats 5–12** | any |
| 20 | seats 18–20 | seats 13–20 | any |
| 24 | seats 22–24 | seats 17–24 | any |

Add a rupee ceiling alongside the multiple, so a Rs 50,000 installment cannot
sneak through on the multiple alone — `L(k) ≤ min(band multiple × c, band rupee

**Tenure quarantine sits on top of all of it and overrides everything.** Every new
member, whatever their score, takes one of the last three seats of any circle of

Rs 20,000 on a Rs 120,000 pot.

**Graduation, as specified:**

```

Decent (550–649)                 →  seats per the L(k) ≤ 7c rule above
```

The requirement to complete **one of each** is a genuinely good control and worth
keeping: a known circle proves you have a real social network, an open circle
proves you pay strangers. Neither alone proves both.

# 6 · HYPER COMMITTEES

## 6.1 · What it is

Daily contributions. Large volume. **Random queue. You do not know anyone else in
the circle and you cannot see them.** No host — Halqa runs the queue.

## 6.2 · The honest risk statement, first

Anonymity removes the single strongest enforcement mechanism a ROSCA has. The
reason a committee works is Kamran's finding, from a housemaid on Rs 15,000 a
month: *"We can compromise on rent or bills, but Committee instalments should not
be missed."* That ranking exists because the group is **known**. Strip the
identities out and what remains is an unsecured 30-day advance to a stranger with
a score penalty as the only consequence.

**Therefore HYPER must be the most heavily gated product on the platform, not the
most open one.** Entry:

```
Sakh ≥ 650
2 completed circles, clean
verified income (payslip or proven salary pattern)
AND, from Stage 2:  a pledged Safety Vault covering L(k) at the claimed seat
```

That last line is the real product. **In HYPER, the pledge is the collateral that
the social bond provides everywhere else.** A member whose pledged units cover
their forward liability cannot default up to the size of that pledge. Until
Stage 2 exists, HYPER runs small and gated as below.

## 6.3 · Charge much more — but first, the arithmetic that governs how much

You asked to charge much higher fees on HYPER. You can, and you should, but the
constraint is not our appetite and it is not the member's willingness. It is
that **daily cadence turns every rupee of fee into a very large annualised rate,
and that annualised rate is the exact number a hostile journalist or an SECP
reviewer will compute.** The 400 banned lending apps were killed by that number.

The early seat in a daily circle is a genuinely short loan:

```
Seat 1, n rounds of c.  Receives n·c on day 1 having paid c.
Net advance          = (n−1)c
Amortises by c/day to zero over (n−1) days
Average outstanding  = (n−1)c / 2
```

So for an all-in member cost `F` at seat 1:

```
                       F                 365            2 × 365 × F
APR-equivalent  =  ───────────────  ×  ───────  =  ───────────────────
                   (n−1)c / 2           n−1            (n−1)² × c
```

Worked, Rs 500/day, 30-day cycle, pot Rs 15,000:

| All-in cost to seat 1 | As % of pot | **APR-equivalent** |
|---|---|---|
| Rs 276 | 1.84% | **48%** |
| Rs 450 | 3.0% | **78%** |
| Rs 750 | 5.0% | **130%** |
| Rs 1,500 | 10.0% | **260%** |

Rs 1,500 on a Rs 15,000 pot feels like a modest 10%. It is **260% APR**. That is
nano-lender territory and it ends the company. This is the single most important
number in the HYPER design and it is why "just charge more" cannot be executed
naively.

**Proposed hard internal ceiling: 48% APR-equivalent at the earliest seat,
all-in.** Justification — it sits above what a microfinance bank charges, far
below anything that reads as predatory, it is a number we can publish, and it is
the standard responsible-finance line. Everything the earliest seat pays counts
toward it: cover premium, access fee, and any time-value premium they pay to
other members. Halqa's own cut is not the test; **the borrower's total cost is
the test**, because that is what gets computed against us.

## 6.4 · The derived consequence — HYPER cycles must be at least 48 days

The risk-priced cover premium (§5.2, `r ≈ 1.5q`, q ≈ 2% for an anonymous circle)
is 3.0% of each installment. Over `n` installments that is `0.03 × n × c`, and
the pot is `n·c`, so the premium is **always 3.0% of the pot, whatever `n` is.**

The APR ceiling as a share of pot, however, grows with `n`:

```
ceiling as % of pot  =  0.48 × (n−1)² / (730 × n)
```

Setting the two equal and solving `0.48(n−1)² = 21.9n` gives **n ≈ 47.6**.

| Cycle | Ceiling as % of pot | Risk-priced premium | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 days | 0.90% | 3.0% | **impossible** |
| 30 days | 1.84% | 3.0% | **breaches by 1.16%** |
| **48 days** | 3.03% | 3.0% | breakeven — the floor |
| **60 days** | **3.81%** | 3.0% | **works**, 0.81% headroom — the ceiling, by direction |

**The chairman has capped HYPER at 60 days.** Combined with the 48-day floor,
that leaves a narrow and fully determined window: **HYPER runs at 48 to 60 days,
and the recommended setting is 60.** Anything shorter cannot be both risk-priced
and non-predatory; anything longer stops being a short-cycle product.

**A 30-day HYPER cannot be both risk-priced and non-predatory.** That is a hard
result, not an opinion. Three ways out, and I recommend the first two together:

1. **Run at 60 days, the maximum allowed.** Doubling the duration from 30 doubles
   the fee you can charge in rupees at the same APR, and 60 is the only setting
   that clears the floor with any headroom at all.
2. **Collateralise with the Safety Vault pledge** (`STAGE-2`). A pledged member
   cannot default up to the pledge, so `q` collapses and the premium with it —
   which is what makes a genuine 15 or 30-day HYPER possible later.
3. **Raise the ceiling and defend it on substance.** The member's real
   alternative is an informal lender at 10–20% a month, i.e. 120–240% APR, so a
   90% product is a large improvement for them. True, and indefensible in a
   headline. **Your ruling, not mine** — I would not take it.

```
Raast                                                 ≈  Rs 0
```

**HYPER is not a product on wallet rails. It is a product on Raast or it does not
exist.** Until SBP's pull scheme ships, HYPER runs on Raast RTP one-tap push with
a same-day auto-cover backstop for anyone who misses the tap — which is exactly
your instinct that HYPER "needs the Raast auto-deduct feature." Confirmed, and
made a creation-time gate: a HYPER circle cannot instantiate unless every member
has a verified Raast credential on file.

## 6.8 · Queue mechanics — the part that has never been specified

HYPER has no roster and no host, so it needs a matching engine. This is the
substantive difference from every other circle type in the product.

**Intake.** A member joins a **pool**, not a circle. One pool per
`(ticket tier × cycle length)`. The join screen shows their queue position and
the projected start date, and nothing about anyone else.

**Instantiation.** When a pool reaches `n` eligible members, a circle instantiates
and the commit–reveal ballot runs immediately. Members are notified with their
seat number, their collection date, and their forward liability in rupees.

**Assignment guards, applied before instantiation:**
- **No two device- or IP-clustered accounts in the same circle.** With anonymity,
  the only collusion attack left is stacking a circle with your own accounts;
  clustering at assignment kills it.
- **No two accounts sharing a referral edge, a guarantor edge, or an address
  cluster** in the same circle. Same reason.
- **Band-and-tenure sort**: seats in the restricted zone are drawn only from the
  eligible subset, so the ballot is random *within* what each member may hold.

**Anonymity, in implementation, not in intention.** `CommitteeMember` gains
`displayAlias` (`"Member #7"`). The members endpoint for a HYPER circle returns
alias, seat, and payment status **only** — it never joins `User`, so no name,
photo, phone, city or Sakh crosses the wire. There is no profile link, no chat
thread, and no export. This is enforced at the query, not in the UI, so a
client-side inspection reveals nothing.

**Payout.** Single daily settlement at 09:00 PKT, Raast, to the member's own
verified account. Netting per §2.6 applies, so a member's own installment for
their collection day is deducted and the payout is `(n−1)c`.

**Substitution and the standing queue.** A member who cannot continue is replaced
from the pool the same day. This is HYPER's structural advantage over every other
circle type: **Level 2 substitution is always available, because a queue always
exists.** There is no group vote, no restitution arithmetic, and no exit ladder —
the leaver is made whole immediately by the substitute, exactly as the ladder
intends, and the circle never contracts.

**Rejoin.** On clean completion, the next cycle is offered automatically with
queue priority. This is the retention loop, and it is the reason HYPER's
lifetime value per member is high despite the small ticket.

## 6.9 · Default handling on a daily clock

The standing grace formula, `clamp(round(0.23 × period), 2, 14) − 2`, returns
**zero** when the period is one day. It must be special-cased before HYPER ships
or every member is late by construction.

```
Grace                 12 hours
T+12h   cover fires   Halqa pays the round recipient. Recipient is NEVER late.
                      Member owes principal + 2% instant-cover fee + 5% late fee.
T+36h   escalation    10% late fee, WhatsApp + push + Raast one-tap request
T+60h   escalation    15% late fee, final notice
3 consecutive misses  removal, substitution from the pool, recovery case opened,
                      Sakh −200 if post-payout, platform-wide lock
```

The recipient never waits. That is the whole promise of a covered circle, and on
a daily clock it has to be automatic — there is no time for a human in the loop.

## 6.10 · The distribution answer nobody has raised

The natural home for HYPER is not the open internet. It is **daily-wage
workplaces** — a factory, a market association, a transport depot, a large
retailer's floor staff. The employer becomes the **institutional anchor**: it
verifies identity and income, the payment day is certain, and the anonymity
problem disappears because the anchor, not the members, carries the trust.

This is also the cheapest possible acquisition channel — one relationship, four
hundred members — and it is the same institutional-anchor mechanism already
specified for workplace circles that fail the authenticity test. It converts the
employer-committee idea from "for later" into HYPER's launch channel.

# 7 · THE VAULT

## 7.1 · The structure

No bank at any point. Under the NBFC Regulations 2008 the assets of every
regulated mutual fund in Pakistan sit with a **trustee**; the AMC only manages
them.

```
member  →  trustee collection account  →  fund units in the member's OWN name
                                       →  redemption to the member's OWN account
```

Halqa never appears in that path. Halqa's registration is an **SECP mutual-fund
distributor registration** — not EMI, because no balances are issued; not NBFC,
because no funds are deployed by us. Investment-adviser licensing is avoided by
presenting an execution-only menu and recommending nothing, ever, in the UI.

## 7.2 · The three products

| Product | Mechanism | Member benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Growth Vault** | Voluntary savings into an Islamic money-market or income fund, ~10–11% against 11–12% inflation | Money that earned zero now earns something |
| **Safety Vault** | The same holding, **pledged**, with a lien in favour of the circle. A miss settles from the member's own units. | **A member with a pledged buffer cannot default up to its size** |
| **The Float** | Contributions buy units in the payer's own name on pay-in, redeem on payout day (§2.2) | Small — Rs 207 per round on a Rs 120,000 pot. Present it as a benefit, never as revenue. |

## 7.3 · The liquidity requirement — and the invariant

Pulling on the 9th and paying on the 10th requires a fund with same-day or T+1
redemption. Islamic daily-dividend money-market funds are built for exactly this,
which is why they are the right instrument. But the design must not depend on it:

**Hard invariant, tested: no payout ever waits on a redemption.** The 10th is
funded from the AMC settlement account or the cover facility, and the redemption
settles behind it. A payout that is late because a fund did not settle would
destroy more trust in one morning than the float earns in a decade.

## 7.4 · Safety Vault lien mechanics — exact

Vague pledges are worthless. The instrument is:

1. A **pledge/charge over units**, registered with the trustee, in favour of the
   circle's members — never in favour of Halqa, matching how the mutual PG is
   already written.
2. An **irrevocable redemption mandate** signed at pledge time, naming the
   trigger (a confirmed missed contribution) and the beneficiary (the round's
   recipient).
3. On a confirmed miss: **Halqa instructs, the AMC redeems, the trustee pays the
   round's recipient directly.** Halqa never receives the money, never holds it,
   and never decides discretionarily — the trigger is mechanical.
4. The pledge releases automatically at circle completion, or steps down as
   forward liability falls.

This is a counsel item. It is also the single most valuable legal document in the
Stage-2 stack, because it is the mechanism that converts Akif's *"if a member
keeps money in it, they can't default"* from a sentence into an enforceable one.

## 7.5 · Where the Stage-2 revenue actually is

```
Float profit share, Halqa 10%          ≈ Rs 249 per circle-cycle
Distributor trail, 0.5–1.5% p.a. of AUM
  at Rs 1bn of vault AUM, 1% trail     = Rs 10,000,000 per year
```

The trail is roughly forty thousand times the float per unit of effort. **Build
the Vault for the trail and for the default cure; treat the float as a feature.**

# 8 · INSTALLMENT COMMITTEES AND THE OWNERSHIP PROBLEM

## 8.1 · The requirement

> *"Need a way so complete ownership doesn't go to the user for the full cycle
> period — even at turn 1, he gets it but it can be withdrawn somehow. Not by us
> necessarily. Just take away from the guy."*

This is the sharpest question in the brief, and it has a clean answer that does
not require Halqa to own, hold, finance or repossess anything.

## 8.2 · The mechanism in one line

**The member takes possession at their turn. Title is held by a third party for
exactly as long as the member still owes the group, and transfers to the member
when the cycle ends.**

**Correction to the first draft.** That draft used **ijarah muntahia bittamleek**
— a lease ending in ownership — and the cash flow does not work. In an ijarah the
lessor must actually receive the rentals. But the pot pays the dealer in full at
round `k`, and the member's remaining contributions go **into the circle to fund
other members' pots**, not to a lessor. Calling those payments "rentals" would be
a fiction, and a financing structure that only works if you do not look at where
the money goes is not a structure. The correct instrument is a **security
interest**, not a lease. Ijarah remains available as a variant (§8.3b) but it
requires a third party to actually fund the asset, which changes who carries the
risk.

**The correct instrument: a conditional sale with retention of title, held by a
security trustee for the circle's members.** Possession and use pass to the
member at their turn. **Legal title does not**, and is held back until the
member's remaining obligation to the circle is discharged. Under the Sale of
Goods Act 1930 property in goods passes when the parties intend it to, and a
seller may reserve the right of disposal until conditions are met — so this is
ordinary Pakistani commercial law, not an exotic structure. It is also exactly
what you described: *the owner owns the item while payments run, and then the
owner is the user.*

The retention period is not an arbitrary policy. It is the forward liability:

```
Member at seat k in an n-round circle
Forward liability at collection      L(k) = c × (n − k)
Retention period                          = (n − k) rounds  →  runs to the cycle's end
Discharge condition                       = the member's own remaining contributions
Total still owed at collection            = c × (n − k) = L(k)
Title releases                             when the member's own obligation reaches zero
Seat n (last)                              L(n) = 0 → nothing owed → release at circle close
```

**The retention is the forward liability, made physical.** Nothing changes for
the member operationally — they keep paying the same `c` on the same day into the
same circle — but if they stop, there is now a thing that can be taken, and the
proceeds go to the eleven people they left short.

## 8.3 · The security trustee, and why one is needed

The dealer is paid in full at round `k`, so **the dealer has no reason to enforce
anything afterwards.** The party with the interest is the circle. So the retained
title cannot sit with the dealer — it has to sit with someone holding it *for the
circle*.

```
Dealer sells under a conditional sale, title reserved
   │
   ├─ assigns the reserved title / right of disposal to
   │
   ▼
SECURITY TRUSTEE  ── holds title for the benefit of the circle's members
   │                 releases it to the member when their obligation hits zero
   │                 enforces and sells it if they default; proceeds → the circle
   ▼
Member has possession and use from day one. Ownership at the end.
```

**The trustee is the answer to "not by us necessarily."** Halqa does not hold
title, does not enforce, and does not receive proceeds. And this is the same
institutional slot as the vault: **CDC and DCC both act as security trustees**,
which is why §12 matters twice.

**Registration, per asset class.** For vehicles, Pakistani practice already has
the mechanism: registration is issued in the financier's name, or in the member's
name with a **hire-purchase / charge endorsement recorded with Excise &
Taxation**. That endorsement is what makes repossession an administrative act
rather than a lawsuit. For serial-numbered machinery, a charge is registered
against the serial. For everything without a registry, see §8.6 — retention is
legally real and practically weak, and it must be priced that way rather than
pretended away.

## 8.3b · The ijarah variant, if you want the Shariah label

An ijarah is still available, but only if a **modaraba actually funds the asset**
rather than the pot:

1. The modaraba buys the asset and owns it. It leases it to the member for `n`
   rounds at rental `c`.
2. The member's committee pot, when it arrives at round `k`, goes to the modaraba
   as an **advance rental**.
3. The member's continuing `c` payments go to the **modaraba** as rentals, and
   **the modaraba pays `c` into the circle on the member's behalf.**
4. So the modaraba carries the member's credit risk, secured by an asset it owns,
   and the circle is made whole regardless of what the member does.
5. Title transfers at term end by **wa'd** → **hiba** or token sale, in three
   separate documents (a sale and a lease merged into one contract is invalid).
6. Because the modaraba genuinely owns: it bears total loss, insures with
   **takaful**, and **rent suspends if the asset becomes unusable through no
   fault of the member** — a financier who charges regardless of whether the
   thing works is charging interest and will be read that way.

This is a stronger product — the circle carries no risk at all on that seat — and
a harder sale, because the modaraba has to underwrite every member. **Recommend
shipping the trustee/retention structure first and offering the ijarah variant
where a modaraba will write it.**

## 8.4 · The money and title flow, step by step

Circle: 12 members × Rs 10,000/month. Pot Rs 120,000. Asset: a motorcycle at
Rs 120,000. Member takes **seat 3**.

```
Formation      Circle created against a NAMED asset from the partner catalogue.
               In the 24h window the member signs: committee agreement,
               conditional sale with retention of title, and the trust deed
               naming the security trustee.

Rounds 1–2     Member pays Rs 10,000 × 2 as ordinary contributions.

ROUND 3        Pot of Rs 120,000 is paid BY THE CIRCLE DIRECT TO THE DEALER.
               (This is Goal Direct-Pay, which already exists.)
               Dealer delivers the bike to the member — POSSESSION AND USE.
               Dealer reserves title and assigns it to the SECURITY TRUSTEE.
               Registration: trustee's name, or member's name with a charge
               endorsement recorded at Excise & Taxation.

               Retention period  = 12 − 3 = 9 rounds
               Discharged by     = the member's own remaining contributions
               Amount            = Rs 90,000  =  L(3), exactly

Rounds 4–12    Member pays Rs 10,000 into the circle exactly as before. Nothing
               about the payment changes. Each one reduces L(k) by Rs 10,000 and
               is posted by Halqa to the trustee as a discharge record.

CIRCLE CLOSE   L(3) = 0. Trustee releases. Title transfers to the member.
               THE MEMBER NOW OWNS THE BIKE.
```

**Coverage improves every month, which is the property that makes this safe.**
The liability falls by `c` per round while the asset depreciates far more slowly:

| Month | Forward liability `L(k)` | Bike's approximate value | Cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Rs 90,000 | Rs 118,000 | 1.31× |
| 6 | Rs 60,000 | Rs 112,000 | 1.87× |
| 9 | Rs 30,000 | Rs 106,000 | 3.53× |
| 12 | **Rs 0** | Rs 100,000 | title transfers |

Compare that to the cash circle, where the exposure is uncollateralised for the
whole nine months. **The installment circle is structurally the safest product in
the range**, and it is the only one where the security appreciates relative to
the debt.

## 8.5 · Everyone receives ownership on the same day — how

This is the question you asked, and it has a clean answer, because **every
member's retention is discharged by their own contributions, and every member's
contributions end on the same round.**

```
Seat 1   collects round  1, owes L(1) = 11c, discharged by round 12
Seat 3   collects round  3, owes L(3) =  9c, discharged by round 12
Seat 8   collects round  8, owes L(8) =  4c, discharged by round 12
Seat 12  collects round 12, owes L(12) =  0, discharged at collection
                                             ─────────────────────────
                                    ALL DISCHARGED AT ROUND 12
```

Different possession dates, **one release date.** The product line is: *"You get
your bike when your turn comes. Everyone gets their papers on the same day — the
day the circle closes."*

**The Circle Close event, specified.**

```
Round n settles                     the final pot releases
+7 days   CLOSE WINDOW              reversals, disputes and late items settle
CLOSE DAY                           one instruction file goes to the trustee:
                                    every member, asset, serial/registration,
                                    and their discharge status
                                    ──────────────────────────────────────
                                    Trustee releases retained title for every
                                    member whose L(k) = 0, in one batch.
Halqa issues                        a signed, verifiable Ownership Certificate
                                    per member — same infrastructure as the
                                    Credit Passport — plus the transfer receipt.
Registration update                 filed the same day. It COMPLETES in days to
                                    weeks for vehicles. Say so; do not promise
                                    the registration book on close day.
```

Seat 12 has a seven-day retention rather than none. That is a formality, and it
is worth keeping, because it makes the document set, the timeline and the story
identical for all twelve members.

**Three properties that must hold, and this is where the design earns its keep:**

1. **No member's title depends on any other member's behaviour.** Release is
   conditional on `L(k) = 0` for that member alone. If member 7 defaults, members
   1–6 and 8–12 still receive title on close day, on time. A structure where one
   person's default strips another person's property is indefensible and would be
   struck down; do not build it, however tempting the alignment looks.
2. **A member who finishes early finishes early.** If someone clears their
   remaining contributions ahead of schedule, their release is available then —
   the batch is a convenience, not a lock. See §8.7 early buyout.
3. **If the circle itself collapses, each retention still stands on its own.**
   The conditional sale is bilateral between the dealer, the trustee and that
   member. A member who kept paying gets their title even if the circle around
   them failed. The asset track survives circle failure, which is a genuine
   advantage over the cash product and should be said out loud.

## 8.5b · What happens on default

```
Member misses         normal ladder runs: reminders, late fee, Sakh damage
Enforcement trigger   after 3 missed contributions the TRUSTEE enforces under
                      the trust deed. Halqa does not decide this and cannot.
Repossession          the trustee recovers the asset. Title is held by the
                      trustee, or the charge is endorsed on the registration,
                      so this is an administrative act, not litigation.
Sale and settlement   the asset is sold. Proceeds settle, in this order:
                        1. the member's outstanding L(k) → PAID INTO THE CIRCLE,
                           which completes the remaining rounds for everyone else
                        2. recovery and sale costs
                        3. THE SURPLUS RETURNS TO THE MEMBER
Halqa's part          records the event, files the credit event, assembles the
                      evidence pack. Halqa never repossesses, never sells,
                      never holds proceeds.
```

**This is the material improvement over the ijarah draft.** Under the trustee
structure the sale proceeds go **to the circle**, so the other eleven members are
made whole out of the defaulter's own asset. Under an ijarah the proceeds would
make the *lessor* whole and the circle would still need the cover facility. The
retention structure secures the right party.

The surplus return is not generosity, it is necessary: the member paid for that
asset with their own contributions, so anything above what they owe is theirs. A
structure that keeps everything is a penalty structure and it will be attacked as
one — by a court and by a journalist.

**Residual gap.** If the sale realises less than `L(k)` — a written-off bike, a
disappeared asset — the shortfall falls to the cover facility (§9.3) and the
mutual PG, exactly as in a cash circle. The asset reduces the exposure; it does
not always extinguish it, and the model should not claim otherwise.

## 8.6 · Where this works, and where it does not

The honest constraint is **recovery is only as good as the registry.**

| Asset class | Title retention | Recovery in practice | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motorcycles, cars, rickshaws, loaders** | Registration in the trustee's name, or a charge endorsement at Excise & Taxation | **Strong.** Already standard practice for leased vehicles. | **Launch here** |
| **Machinery, generators, shop fittings, sewing and embroidery machines** | Serial-numbered, charge registered against the serial | Good | **Launch here** |
| **Phones and tablets** | Title passes but the device locks remotely on non-payment — standard emerging-market handset financing | Moderate | Second wave |
| **Fridges, ACs, furniture** | No registry exists | **Weak.** Retention is legally real and practically unenforceable. | Price for it, or run these as ordinary cash goal circles |
| **Cash** (no asset) | none possible | — | **Out of scope.** Cash circles are secured by `L(k)`, position, PG and the Safety Vault. Say so plainly rather than implying every circle has an asset behind it. |

**Do not launch on unregistrable assets.** The temptation is fridges and phones,
because that is what people ask for. The security only works where a registry or
a lock exists, so bikes and machines are the launch set, and everything else runs
as an ordinary cash goal circle with no ownership claim attached to it. Promising
retention on a sofa is a promise that cannot be kept.

## 8.7 · Early buyout, and the member's own options

The member does not own the asset, so they cannot sell it, pledge it, or export
it — which is the point. What they *can* do:

- **Buy out early.** Pay the remaining contributions in one payment; `L(k)` hits
  zero, the trustee releases, and title transfers immediately without waiting for
  circle close. Charge a **1% early-transfer fee** on the amount cleared, and no
  more — a large early-settlement penalty is exactly the abuse that
  consumer-conduct rules target.
- **Substitute out.** If they exit the circle under Level 2, the substitute
  assumes the seat **and** the retained-title obligation, subject to the same
  `seatEligible` check plus the asset's own affordability test.
- **Hand back.** Voluntary surrender before default: the asset returns, sale
  proceeds clear `L(k)` into the circle, the surplus returns to the member, and
  there is **no default flag and no −200**. This is the hardship path with an
  asset attached, and it should exist — it is the cheapest possible resolution
  for everyone and it must not be punished into disuse.

## 8.8 · Halqa's position, stated for the regulator

```
Halqa does NOT own the asset          the security trustee holds title,
                                      for the circle's members
Halqa does NOT hold title             registration sits with the trustee, or
                                      carries a charge endorsement
Halqa does NOT finance                the CIRCLE funds the purchase, from members
Halqa does NOT repossess              the trustee does, under the trust deed
Halqa does NOT receive proceeds       they go to the circle, then to the member
Halqa does NOT take credit risk       beyond the published cover facility (§9.3)
Halqa DOES                            introduce, schedule, collect, record,
                                      post discharge records, file credit events,
                                      and assemble the evidence pack
```

That keeps Halqa outside the NBFC regime, which attaches to lending and to
pooling-and-deploying, and outside the leasing regime, which attaches to owning
and leasing. **Two counsel items to close before launch:** whether introducing a
member to a dealer and a trustee for a commission constitutes arranging credit as
a regulated activity in Pakistan; and confirmation that a retention-of-title
assignment to a trustee for the benefit of a group of individuals is enforceable
as drafted. I do not believe the first is a problem at this scale, but both
should be opinions on file rather than my view.

Two roles, and they can be two different organisations:

| Role | Who | Why |
|---|---|---|

# 9 · THE BUSINESS MODEL

## 9.1 · The complete fee book

Members pay **Rs 0** to join a circle and **Rs 0** to pay an installment,
permanently. Every charge below is on an *optional priced event* or is paid by a
counterparty. That boundary is what makes the Rs 0 promise true rather than
marketing.

**A · Member-borne, per round.** Hard ceiling: total per payment
`≤ min(1.5% of the installment, Rs 300)`.

| Charge | Open | Known | HYPER |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cover premium | 1.5%, cap Rs 300 | 0.5% optional, cap Rs 150 | 3.0% (disclosed in rupees) |

HYPER's 3% exceeds the standard envelope deliberately — it is the price of
anonymity — and it is governed by a second ceiling that binds harder than the
Rs 300 rule: **all-in cost to the earliest seat must stay under 48%
APR-equivalent** (§6.3). That ceiling is what forces HYPER onto 60 and 90-day
cycles rather than 15 and 30, and it is checked at circle creation. Disclosure on
the join screen is in rupees, never in percent: *"This circle costs you Rs 1,080
over 60 days."*

**B · Pot-side, paid by the collector out of the pot.**

| Charge | Formula | Worked (Rs 120,000 pot, n=12) |
|---|---|---|
| Early-seat fee | `base × (n − k)/(n − 1)`; base 100 bps open, 175 known, 250 HYPER | Open seat 1 → **Rs 1,200**; seat 6 → **Rs 654**; seat 12 → **Rs 0** |
| Salary-verified discount | ×0.8 | Open seat 1 verified → **Rs 960** |
| Slot fee (Halqa fills a vacant seat) | **Rs 500 flat**, ×0.8 if verified | Rs 500 / Rs 400 |
| Marketplace, sponsor and auction commission | **10% of the accepted premium** | Rs 5,000 premium → **Rs 500** |

**C · Event-driven.**

| Charge | Open | Known |
|---|---|---|
| Late ladder, of the installment | 3% / 7% / 12% | 1% / 3% / 6% |
| Exit fine | 1 installment · **70% to remaining members, 30% Halqa** | same |
| Rehabilitation fee on recovery | 10% of outstanding | 10% |

On Shariah-labelled circles late fees route to the **circle's own pool**, not to
Halqa. A fixed penalty retained as income is impermissible; passed to the injured
members it is compensation.

**D · Counterparty-paid.**

| Line | Rate | Payer |
|---|---|---|
| Goal Direct-Pay commission | **1–3%** of brokered value | Merchant |
| Ijarah origination referral | **2–4%** of asset value | Modaraba / merchant |
| Vault distributor trail | **0.5–1.5% p.a.** of AUM | AMC |

## 9.2 · The spin, the ad, and the line I would draw

> *"Fees on installment, daily spin with very little chance to win money, watch an
> ad to unlock another. Ad monetisation HERE ONLY."*

**"Here only" is exactly right** and it should be a written rule. Advertising
anywhere else in a financial app — on the payment screen, on the circle screen, in
notifications — trades the trust moat for pennies.

**The legal shape.** A prize with a money value is a free-entry promotion, not
gambling, only while there is **no consideration to enter**. Payment, purchase, or
a fee makes it a lottery under the Prevention of Gambling Act and its provincial
successors. Watching an ad is consideration in several readings. So split it:

```
FREE DAILY SPIN     no entry cost, no ad required  →  may carry a cash prize
AD-UNLOCKED SPIN    watching an ad is the entry    →  NON-CASH ONLY
                                                      (points, fee waivers, vouchers)
```

**The Shariah shape.** A prize funded entirely by the platform as *hiba*, with no
participant contribution, is permissible — this is already how the prize *hiba* in
`sukoon.ts` is framed. A draw participants pay into is *maysir*. The split above
satisfies both tests with the same design.

**The reputational shape, and this is the part I would push back on.** A
spin-to-win wheel inside a savings product aimed at low-income Pakistanis is the
highest-risk element in the entire brief. It rhymes with the **prize committee**
this model formally refused as *"a lottery wearing a committee's clothes"*, and a
wheel on a savings app is a five-second screenshot for a hostile story.

**What I would build instead, and it monetises identically:** the same reward
budget, delivered as **deterministic streak milestones** — day 7, day 30, day 90,
circle completed — with the rewarded video as the unlock for the *bonus* tier.
Same ad inventory, same daily-return loop, no wheel, no chance, no legal question,
no headline.

**If the wheel stays** — and under the standing rule it may — the five guards are:
(i) never on the payment or circle screens; (ii) cash prizes **credit against
future installments**, never into a wallet, which also keeps us clear of e-money;
(iii) no near-miss animation and no losing animation; (iv) published odds and a
published prize budget; (v) a hard daily cap on prize spend.

**Ad revenue, honestly sized.** Rewarded-video eCPM in Pakistan is roughly
**$1.5–4 per 1,000 completed views** (verify against a live mediation account).

```
100,000 DAU · 30% watch one rewarded video a day  =  30,000 views/day
                                                  ≈  900,000 views/month
                                                  ≈  $1,350–3,600/month
                                                  ≈  Rs 380,000–1,000,000/month
```

Real, and worth taking. Not a business, and it cannot be scaled by adding
surfaces, because there is only one surface by rule.

## 9.3 · The cover facility — and the answer to the open tension

The decision log records an unresolved tension: **Akif says cover defaults on the
balance sheet and insure with takaful; the competitor archive says a platform
guarantee is precisely what killed eMoneyPool.** Both are right, and the
reconciliation is structural rather than a choice between them.

**An unlimited guarantee is fatal. A bounded, priced, subrogated insurance
product is ordinary.** Four properties convert one into the other:

1. **Priced.** The premium is `r ≈ 1.5q` per §5.2 — not free, not bundled, not
   silent. eMoneyPool's guarantee was free, which meant it was unpriced, which
   meant it was unbounded.
2. **Capped, at four levels, all published:**
   ```
   per circle          ≤ 1 × pot
   per host per month  ≤ Rs 500,000
   per member          ≤ their own forward liability, never more
   aggregate monthly   ≤ 2% of Halqa's paid-up capital
   ```
3. **Subrogated.** Cover is **an advance to the defaulter, not a gift.** It opens a
   `RecoveryCase` for the full amount plus the 10% rehabilitation fee, and Halqa
   stands in the circle's shoes. This is the whole difference from eMoneyPool.
4. **Reinsured above the aggregate cap** by a takaful operator, which is exactly
   what Akif meant by insuring the exposure. Below the attachment point Halqa
   retains; above it, the operator does.

With those four in place, the answer to *"do you cover defaults?"* is: **"Yes, up
to a published cap, for a price, and we recover it."** That answer satisfies the
Chairman, survives the archive's objection, and is the version I would take back
to him for the ruling that has never been asked for.

## 9.4 · Set-off against secondary products

> *"Any defaults by user, money pulled from other secondary products such as vault
> and installment-based committee."*

Mechanically, in strict order:

```
1. Safety Vault pledged units       → redeem, pay the round recipient directly   (§7.4)
2. Growth Vault unpledged units     → only under a separate signed set-off clause,
                                      and only after notice with a 7-day cure period
3. Pot deductions in live circles   → the §1.5 waterfall, ≤60% cap
4. Sponsor / restitution claims     → assigned to the cover facility on subrogation
5. Ijarah asset                     → the partner terminates and recovers  (§8)
6. Recovery case                    → outstanding + penalties + 10%, Order XXXVII
```

Two rules that must never bend. **The set-off runs in favour of the circle's
members, never in favour of Halqa** — the same construction as the mutual PG.
And **Halqa never seizes anything**: it instructs a trustee under a pre-signed
mandate, or it hands an evidence pack to a partner or a court.

# 10 · CONSUMERS AND SAVERS — THE SPLIT AKIF ASKED FOR

One product has been serving two people with opposite needs. Split them at the
join screen, because everything downstream follows from the answer.

| | **The consumer** | **The saver** |
|---|---|---|
| Wants | The pot **early**, to spend | Discipline, and the pot **late** |
| Instrument taken | An interest-free advance | A commitment device |
| Forward liability | **High** — the whole risk of the model | Falls to zero |
| Terms | PG enforced, undertaking weekly, cover premium mandatory, salary anchor required, seat gated by band and tenure, early-seat fee applies | Lighter friction, no early-seat fee |
| Upsell | Goal Direct-Pay, ijarah asset, credit-builder | **Growth Vault, Safety Vault, float share** |
| Revenue | Early-seat fee, merchant commission | **Distributor trail** |
| The question asked at join | *"What do you need the money for, and when?"* | *"What are you saving towards?"* |

The saver is where the AUM and therefore the durable revenue is. The consumer is
where the risk and the merchant commission is. **A circle that is all consumers
should not be allowed to form** — a healthy roster needs late seats that people
actually want, which is precisely what the vault's yield makes possible for the
first time.

# 11 · WHAT I WOULD ADD

Ranked by what I think each is worth.

1. **Netting the collector's own installment (§2.6).** Rs 3,600–7,200 saved per
   circle-cycle in rail cost, a whole failure class deleted, one day of work.
   Nothing else in this document has that ratio.
2. **The forward-liability ceiling as the single risk primitive (§5.3).** It
   reproduces the chairman's own seat rule, generalises to every circle size,
   and replaces four arbitrary tables with one inequality.
3. **`r ≈ 1.5q` (§5.2).** Every price in the fee book becomes a function of one
   measured number. It also means the pitch can say honestly: *these prices are
   derived, and the first hundred circles set them.*
3b. **The 48% APR ceiling and the 48-day floor on HYPER (§6.3–6.4).** Daily
   cadence makes a modest-looking 10%-of-pot fee into a 260% APR. Because the
   risk-priced premium is always 3.0% of the pot regardless of circle size while
   the ceiling grows with duration, **a HYPER circle shorter than 48 days cannot
   be both risk-priced and non-predatory.** Run 60 and 90-day cycles. This one
   result changes the product's shape, not just its price.
4. **The bounded, subrogated cover facility (§9.3).** It resolves the one open
   tension in the decision log, and it is the difference between an insurance
   product and the mistake that killed eMoneyPool.
5. **Sponsor / pay-on-behalf (§1.4).** The best growth loop in the brief. It
   turns a would-be default into a member-funded save and it brings
   non-members onto the platform to do it.
6. **The verifiable commit–reveal ballot (§3.5).** Kills the oldest complaint
   about ballot committees, costs a day, and is a genuine differentiator no
   wallet will bother to build.
7. **Employers as HYPER's launch channel (§6.6).** One relationship, four hundred
   members, and the anonymity problem solved by an anchor rather than by price.
8. **Credit-life takaful.** Death and disability are real causes of committee
   collapse and cover is cheap — roughly **0.2–0.5% of exposure**. On a
   Rs 120,000 pot that is Rs 240–600 for the whole cycle. It removes the single
   most sympathetic default case, and *"if you die, your family owes nothing"* is
   the strongest trust line available to a company that cannot yet promise
   returns.
9. **A second trustee.** Being single-sourced on CDC is a commercial risk before
   it is a technical one. Qualify a second (§12).
10. **The deterministic streak instead of the wheel (§9.2).** Same revenue, same
    retention loop, none of the legal or reputational surface.
11. **No published addresses (§3.6).** The one thing in the brief I would refuse,
    with a stronger substitute in its place.
