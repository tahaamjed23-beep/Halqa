1 · The informal savings market

Committees, known across Pakistan as kameti or BC, are the country's oldest and most widely used savings arrangement. Roughly Rs 4 trillion rotates through them every year, making them the third largest savings channel in the country, and approximately 37 per cent of adults participate, which is some 52 million people. Against this, only 13 per cent of adults hold a bank account, and among those who do save, 63 per cent keep cash at home. The committee therefore is not competing with the banking system; it is competing with a cupboard.

Every one of these arrangements produces a repayment record. A tailor who has paid Rs 8,000 on the first of the month for eleven consecutive months has demonstrated exactly the behaviour a lender spends money trying to establish, and yet that behaviour is recorded nowhere. It exists in a notebook, in a WhatsApp group, or in the memory of the organiser, and when the cycle ends it disappears. Consequently a household which has been financially reliable for a decade arrives at a bank as a stranger.

Two problems follow from this, and they are the two the business addresses. The first is that the arrangement itself is fragile, since it depends entirely on one person holding the money and on the group's memory of who has paid. The second, and the more valuable, is that an entire class of credit data is being generated at national scale and then thrown away.

It is to be noted that the participant is not the destitute household. The academic work on Pakistani committees is consistent that regular income is a precondition of membership, and that groups exclude the irregularly paid themselves. The member is the tailor on Rs 17,000 a month and the driver on Rs 22,000, whose income is modest but arrives on a predictable day. Committees filter on the regularity of income rather than on its level, and that distinction is what makes the resulting record a usable credit signal.

2 · The instrument and the platform

A committee is a fixed arrangement between a group of people. Each member pays the same amount every period, each period the entire pot is paid to one member, and after one full rotation every member has paid the same total and received the same total. Total inflow equals total outflow, so the arrangement requires no lender and no capital. Whoever collects early has received an advance which they repay in instalments at no charge; whoever collects late has saved under a commitment device. The same schedule delivers both, funded by the members themselves.

Halqa records that arrangement. Contributions move directly from the paying member's account to the receiving member's account over Pakistan's domestic payment rails, Halqa observes each settlement, and writes it to a double entry ledger linked to the member's national identity number. The platform therefore performs three functions and no others.

FunctionDescription
The recordEvery settlement carries a receipt, a timestamp and an identity link. Electronic records of this kind are admissible under the Electronic Transactions Ordinance 2002
The treasuryScheduling, pricing, collection and settlement, without holding funds at any point
The credit recordPayment history is converted into a portable score, Sakh, which the member can present outside the platform and which is intended to be furnished to a licensed credit bureau

The strategic position follows from the third of these. Informal committee repayment history exists in no written form anywhere, so at scale Halqa becomes the only supplier of a class of credit data which the national credit system does not presently hold. Bureaux operate on reciprocity, so furnishing data earns access to it in return.

Members pay nothing to join a circle and nothing to make a contribution, and that position is permanent. Revenue is described in section 9 and arises from optional priced services and from counterparties rather than from the member.

3 · Architecture

Halqa does not take possession of member money at any stage. When a member contributes, the funds travel from that member's account to the recipient's account, and the platform holds no balance and has no ability to spend member funds. Second stage savings sit with a licensed trustee in the member's own name.

Two consequences follow. The first is commercial. Every large committee fraud recorded in Pakistan required one precondition, being a central pool of members' money under the control of a single person. Halqa has no such account, so the fraud which destroys committees cannot occur, and the protection cannot be weakened under commercial pressure because there is nothing there to weaken.

The second is that the platform can be operated by a registered company without a financial licence, since it issues no balances, lends nothing, and operates no payment system of its own. The path to real money therefore runs through company registration and a merchant services agreement with a licensed payment aggregator, both of which are commercial steps.

Researchers at Information Technology University Lahore and the University of Washington, funded by the Gates Foundation and Karandaaz Pakistan, published this same architecture in 2018, concluding that funds flowing through a committee application should be directed to an individual's own wallet with the application acting as a conduit. Halqa arrived at the design from the fraud angle and they arrived at it from the regulatory angle, and it is the same architecture.

4 · Model framework

The model is arranged on five pillars, and every feature in the product sits on one of them. The arrangement came out of direct guidance on how a savings and credit model of this kind should be constructed, and it is the structure used in all discussion of the business.

PillarWhat it governs
1OnboardingWho is admitted, what is verified, and what each member is permitted to see. Barriers are kept low and the filtering is invisible, so that a member is shown only the circles they are eligible for and can afford rather than being refused after applying
2CollectionHow money is actually taken. Collection is attempted on the member's payday rather than on the due date, because that is the point in the month at which the balance is highest
3Pooling productsWhere money sits when it is not moving. Halqa holds nothing, so idle savings are routed to a licensed asset manager with a trustee holding the assets in the member's own name
4PreventionThe design principle that recovery machinery is the wrong thing to build. The correct thing to build is an arrangement in which there is no incentive to default, or in which the consequence is severe enough that nobody chooses it
5RecoveryWhat actually happens when prevention fails, expressed as instruments which can be executed rather than as intentions

Two further instructions cut across all five. The first is that consumers and savers are separated at the point of joining, since a member who takes the pot early to spend it and a member who waits in order to save are carrying opposite risks and should not be offered the same terms. The second is that filtering is to be preferred over enforcement in every case where both are available.

5 · Default prevention

The whole of the risk in a committee is a single quantity. At the moment a member collects, they hold the entire pot and still owe every contribution which remains, and that remaining obligation is the forward liability.

L(k) = c ( n − k )     for a member at seat k in a circle of n members paying c

It is largest at the first seat, falls by exactly one contribution with each later seat, and is zero at the last. Risk in a committee is therefore not spread across the members at all; it is concentrated in the early seats and decays to nothing. Consequently the platform does not need a general system of judging members. It needs a system which determines which member may occupy which seat, and every control described below is an application of that.

Exhibit 2. Forward liability by seat. Twelve members, contribution Rs 10,000, pot Rs 120,000.

5.1 Position

Each score band carries a ceiling on the forward liability a member may hold at the point of collection, expressed in contributions. A member in the lowest band may hold no more than two contributions of exposure, which places them in the last three seats. A member in the second band may hold no more than seven, which on a twelve member circle places them at seat five or later. The upper two bands may take any seat.

Above this sits a rule which applies to every new member irrespective of score, since a new account's opening score records nothing. Every new member may take only one of the last three seats of any circle, of any size and any duration, and the restriction is released after two completed circles with clean payment records together with a manual verification step. The worst case exposure to a member the platform has never observed is therefore two contributions, which on a Rs 120,000 pot is Rs 20,000.

5.2 Incentive structure

A member who defaults after collecting gains the forward liability and loses a great deal more. They lose access to the platform, they lose the record which is the reason they joined, they carry a score reduction of 200 points which is visible to every circle they subsequently attempt to enter, and they face a recovery case for the outstanding amount plus penalties plus a rehabilitation fee of 10 per cent. Where an asset circle is involved, the asset itself is recovered by the party holding title.

Alongside this sits the arithmetic of the seat, which is the more important half. A member restricted to the final seats has nothing to gain by defaulting, since by the time they collect they have already paid nearly everything they owe. The design therefore does not attempt to make defaulting difficult for everybody. It ensures that the members with the largest incentive to default are exactly the members who have earned the right not to be treated as a risk.

5.3 Affordability

Most default which is not fraud is over commitment, so three caps are applied at the point of joining and at circle creation, evaluated across every circle the member holds. Total contributions may not exceed 33 per cent of net monthly income; contributions together with known debt service may not exceed 40 per cent; and total forward liability, evaluated at the worst case in which every seat is early, may not exceed four times net monthly income. Concurrency is capped separately, at one circle for a member whose income is unverified and four where it is verified.

The 40 per cent figure anchors to the State Bank's cap on the debt burden ratio for consumer financing, which was reduced from 50 per cent in 2021. Halqa sits stricter than that on contributions alone.

A point of discipline applies here which the literature on progressive lending is consistent about. Escalating limits produce liquidity defaults once the limit outruns real capacity, and the member does not become dishonest so much as over committed. Good payment history therefore unlocks seats, additional circles and reduced friction, and it does not raise the money cap. Only new evidence of income raises the money cap.

5.4 Recovery

Recovery is expressed as instruments rather than as intentions. Every member signs an undertaking and an automatically executed mutual guarantee which runs member to member rather than to Halqa. Both qualify as a written contract for a fixed sum, which permits recovery by summary suit under Order XXXVII of the Code of Civil Procedure, where the defendant has ten days to seek leave to defend and a decree is executable against assets. Where a member holds pledged savings, a missed contribution settles from their own units under a pre signed redemption instruction. Where an asset circle is involved, the party holding title recovers the asset and the proceeds are paid into the circle so that the remaining members are completed.

5.5 The seven layers

The controls described above sit within a stack of seven, each independent of the others, so that no single failure exposes the circle.

6 · Collection

Collection is the first rung of the business, since until contributions settle over a payment rail the ledger is recording assertions rather than settlements, and a record of assertions has no value as credit data.

The collection event is the member's payday rather than the due date. On the evening before the declared payday a balance notice is sent, and on the payday morning, while the balance is at its monthly maximum and before the contribution is even due, the instruction executes. Where it fails, a retry ladder runs to the due date alongside a payment request and a message. Only where every attempt fails does the member meet the late fee ladder. The due date has therefore stopped being the collection event and has become the deadline for resolving failures.

The declared payday is verified from evidence the platform legitimately holds rather than from open banking, which does not exist in Pakistan. The account title returned by the aggregator at linking must match the member's identity card. Every collection attempt is logged against a calendar day, so the first successful collection in each month marks a day on which funds were provably present, and two consistent months establish the payday. Where a member declares the wrong day, the retry ladder finds the correct one. This signal costs nothing and is the strongest of the four available.

Rail economics decide the shape of the product. Members pay nothing, so every rupee of transaction cost is unrecovered.

Halqa reaches Raast as a merchant of a licensed payment aggregator, which is a commercial contract rather than a licence.

7 · Product range

Four circle types are offered, and they differ in who the members are, how heavily the platform verifies them, and who carries the loss where a member stops paying.

7.2 Eligibility, and what a member is shown

The band ceilings apply unchanged across the range, since forward liability on an asset circle is the same quantity as on a cash circle. A member in the lowest band may hold no more than two contributions of exposure and therefore takes one of the last three seats, and a member in the second band no more than seven. The tenure rule applies whatever the score, so a new member takes a last three seat and the worst case exposure to an unproven member is two contributions.

The affordability caps apply to the contribution rather than to the price of the asset, so what a member can reach is set by what they can pay each month and by the length of the circle. The discovery screen shows only those circles and assets for which the member has both an eligible free seat and sufficient headroom, so a member browsing motorcycles is shown the models their income supports and is not shown the others. There is no rejection message, because nothing ineligible is ever displayed.

7.3 The time value spectrum

On known, open and asset circles the same economic difference is priced by a spectrum which the host sets at creation. Where it is enabled, a member taking a seat in the first half pays a premium which is distributed to the members taking later seats in proportion to the time each of them waits, with the premium at the first seat capped at 5 per cent of the pot and Halqa retaining 10 per cent of the pool. Where it is disabled, every seat costs the same. It is forced off on Shariah labelled tiers, where the passage of time may not be priced, and the price is displayed to every member before the confirmation window closes so that seat selection is made against a known figure.

7.4 Savings layer

Committee money earns nothing while it waits, which is a straightforward loss to the member against inflation running at 11 to 12 per cent. The savings layer routes voluntary savings into an Islamic money market fund, with the assets held by a trustee and the units registered in the member's own name, so Halqa still holds nothing.

The same holding, when pledged, produces the strongest default control in the model. A member whose pledged units cover their forward liability cannot default up to the size of that holding, since a missed contribution settles from their own units under an irrevocable redemption instruction signed at the time of pledge. Every member who takes the savings product therefore reduces the exposure of every circle they sit in.

8 · HYPER committees

A HYPER committee takes daily contributions from a fixed roster of four hundred members over a fixed cycle of sixty days. Six or seven members collect on each day, positions are set once by auction, members have no visibility of one another, and there is no organiser. It is the product built for the daily wage earner, who is the largest excluded group in the market and has no savings instrument at all.

8.1 Structure

The roster and the cycle do not vary. Four hundred places are distributed across sixty days, being seven recipients on forty of the days and six on the remaining twenty, which allocates all four hundred exactly and averages 6.67 a day. The daily contribution sets the pot, which is sixty times that contribution and is capped at Rs 15,000.

8.2 The default rate the product can bear

The rate the product absorbs follows from the premium and from the structure, and can be verified directly. Where r is the insurance premium as a share of each contribution, premium income per member over the cycle is sixty contributions multiplied by r, while expected loss per defaulting member at the mean day of collection is 29.5 contributions. Setting the two equal gives the rate at which premium income exactly meets loss.

default rate absorbed by the premium  =  r × 60 ÷ 29.5  =  2.03 r

The premium is set at fifteen per cent of each contribution, absorbing 30.5 per cent on its own. 8.3 Entry conditions

A daily contribution is a different obligation from a monthly one. A member paid on the first of the month does not hold a thousand rupees in a wallet on every day of the following month, so the product admits only members whose income itself arrives daily, or members who have set the whole of the exposure aside in advance. One of the two conditions below must be satisfied.

8.4 The opening auction

Positions are set once, at the start, by auction, and are fixed for the whole of the cycle thereafter. A position may not be sold, bought or altered once the auction has closed, and HYPER committees do not appear on the turn marketplace which operates across the rest of the platform.

The window runs for twenty four hours from the point at which the roster of four hundred completes. A member bids for one single named day of the cycle, may raise that bid as often as they wish while the window is open, and may not bid across several days. The standing highest bid on each day is visible so that a position can be priced, and no bidder is identified. Each day carries six or seven places and the highest bids on that day take them. Members who placed no bid, and members who were outbid, are assigned to the remaining days by verifiable ballot. Only a winning bid is charged, and a bid is refused at the point of placing where the member's band or affordability does not permit the forward liability at that day.

Every premium is paid to Halqa in full. There is no member to member premium and no commission split, so the platform receives the whole of the amount rather than a share of it.

8.5 Collection and cover

Collection runs on Raast under a standing daily instruction, which the arithmetic makes a requirement rather than a preference. At Rs 250 a day across four hundred members over sixty days, wallet debit at 1.5 per cent would produce Rs 90,000 of transaction cost against a pot of Rs 15,000.

Where a member misses a day, Halqa pays that day's recipients from its own balance sheet within twelve hours, so that no recipient waits. The advance opens a recovery case for the contribution, an advance fee of two per cent and a late fee which rises from seven to fourteen to twenty two per cent at twelve, thirty six and sixty hours. Three consecutive misses remove the member, who is replaced from the standing queue so that the roster stays at four hundred, and where the locked savings condition was used at entry the holding is applied against the outstanding amount.

8.6 Daily settlement, and why the product needs no capital

A HYPER committee settles every day rather than once a month. At the top tier it collects Rs 100,000 across the roster and pays the same amount on the same morning, being seven pots of Rs 15,000 on forty of the days and six on the remaining twenty. The payout is fixed and the collection is not, so every member who misses creates a shortfall which Halqa fronts that morning, while the premium is collected on the same daily rhythm and only from members still paying.

The figures above treat every missed contribution as a permanent stop. A proportion of misses are late payments which are subsequently made, and each of those reduces the shortfall and adds a late fee at the same time, so the position stated here is the worst case rather than the expected one.

8.7 Distribution

The channel is workplaces and platforms paying daily or weekly, being factories, market associations, transport depots, ride hailing and delivery fleets, and the floor staff of large retailers. The employer or the platform acts as an institutional anchor, verifying identity and income and fixing the payment day, and a single relationship supplies the whole roster of four hundred.

9 · Retail partnerships and the rewards ladder

9.1 Definition

A rewards ladder attached to consecutive contributions paid on or before the due date, redeemable against discounts and goods at partner retailers, of which the largest in Pakistan is Daraz.

9.2 Purpose

On time payment is the single input on which the whole model rests, since expected loss, the insurance premium, the score, and the value of the data asset all move with it. The rewards ladder attaches a tangible and immediate benefit to the behaviour the business needs, and does so with money which comes from the retailer rather than from Halqa. It also gives the member a reason to open the application between contributions, which is otherwise a product they touch once a month.

9.3 Reward ladder

A streak counts consecutive rounds paid on or before the due date, across all of a member's circles, and a missed round resets it to zero. A contribution paid on a member's behalf by somebody else is neutral and neither builds nor breaks a streak, so that the ladder cannot be bought.

Points buy the platform rewards listed above at published prices, so that a member who does not wish to shop is not excluded from the system.

9.4 Funding

The discount at every retailer tier is the retailer's own promotional discount, offered to an audience which has been qualified by twelve months of demonstrated payment behaviour, and it is therefore customer acquisition spend for them rather than a cost to Halqa. Halqa earns an affiliate commission on the resulting order, which in Pakistani marketplace programmes runs at roughly 3 to 12 per cent of order value depending on category. The arrangement is self funding by construction, since the reward is the retailer's discount and the revenue is the retailer's commission.

The platform rewards on the right of the ladder are waivers of charges Halqa would otherwise collect, so they cost the platform their face value and are used at the tiers where the retention benefit is largest.

Retailers to approach are the general marketplaces, being Daraz and the smaller electronics and home retailers such as Telemart, iShopping and HomeShopping; the grocery chains operating online, being Naheed, Alfatah and Imtiaz; and the delivery platforms. Offline chains follow once volume justifies a negotiated code.

9.6 Constraints

Rewards are never redeemable for cash at any tier, since a cash balance held for a member would be a stored value product and would change the regulatory position of the whole platform. A voucher is retailer credit and sits with the retailer.

The reward is capped as a share of the member's contributions and is released only against contributions which have actually settled, so that the ladder cannot become the reason a member joins a circle they cannot afford. Halqa receives order attribution rather than basket contents, and purchase behaviour is not sold. Categories which conflict with a savings product, being gambling and consumer lending, are excluded from the catalogue.

12.1 What the set establishes

Four observations run through the whole of the evidence and each of them is reflected in the design set out in this document.

Custody is the variable which determines the outcome, and its effect is binary. Every operator which held member money either carried the cost of a licence and a statutory deposit, as the Indian foremen do, or failed, as the concentrated Pakistani case did. Halqa holds nothing at any stage.

The value is in the record and it has been measured. Two of the American cases abandoned the pooling entirely and built businesses on reporting payment behaviour to credit bureaus, one of them reaching a billion dollar valuation on that basis. The committee is the mechanism which generates the record, and the record is the asset.

Distribution is agents rather than advertising. Every case which reached scale did so through commissioned organisers, whether the 65,000 agents of the Indian operator or the community organisers used in Indonesia. The host is therefore treated here as the acquisition channel.

An unpriced guarantee is unbounded. The American operator which absorbed defaults on behalf of every member ran for nine years and closed. The facility in section 5 is priced against a stated default rate, capped at four separate levels, and recovered from the member as an advance rather than written off.

All money is held as integer paisa, every movement is a double entry pair so the ledger is self checking, every write carries an idempotency key so a retried request applies once, and production migrations are additive so old and new code remain correct through a deployment. Passwords are hashed at cost twelve, refresh tokens rotate with reuse detection which burns the whole token family, accounts lock after five failures, and queries are parameterised throughout so injection is structurally unavailable.

15 · Sequence and dependencies

The business is a ladder and the rungs cannot be taken out of order, since each one is reachable only because the one below it exists.

RungWhat it unlocks
1CollectionSettlement rather than assertion. Without it there is no credible record, therefore no data asset and no business. Requires company registration and a merchant services agreement
2The recordVerified repayment at volume, which is the raw material for everything above. Measured in circles completed rather than in registrations
3The bureau linkThe record becomes a formal credit signal, the member gains a benefit they cannot obtain elsewhere, and Halqa becomes the supplier of a data class the system does not hold
4CommerceRetailers and institutions pay for access to outcomes. The rewards ladder, direct payment and asset circles all sit here, and this is where the revenue concentrates
5The savings layerYield for the member, a trail for the platform, and a pledged buffer which cures default at source

The near term work divides into engineering, which is under the company's own control, and counterparty work, which carries lead times measured in months and is therefore started in parallel rather than in sequence. The counterparty items are company registration, the merchant services agreement, identity verification through NADRA, the trustee and asset manager relationships, and the retail affiliate registrations.

Most conventional metrics are misleading for this business, since registrations, downloads and total value committed can all be raised without the underlying arrangement working at all. Five measures determine whether it is working.
