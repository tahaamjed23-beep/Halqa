2 · Three things the research corrected

You sent us to check. Two of these correct assumptions we were carrying, and one corrects something said in the meeting. We would rather bring them to you than let you find them later.

Takaful does not cover default — a credit guarantee does

You told us to insure the default exposure. Takaful operators (EFU, Pak-Qatar, Salaam) cover death and disability only; no takaful or conventional insurer covers wilful default on a savings obligation. The correct instrument is a credit guarantee, and Pakistan has a dedicated vehicle: NCGCL — the National Credit Guarantee Company, established 2024 by Karandaaz with the Ministry of Finance — with a live Rs 2.0bn PMIC–NCGCL portfolio guarantee precedent from January 2026.

So the corrected stack is: Halqa first-loss → NCGCL-style guarantee for the tail → credit-life takaful sitting on top as a member benefit, not as our backstop. This is a better answer than the one we came in with, and we got to it because you pushed on insurance.

Oraan is SECP-licensed

In the meeting the working assumption was that Oraan operates without an SECP licence. It holds an NBFC “Investment Finance Services” licence, SECP/LRD/107/OFSPL/2023, granted 2023; before that it ran inside the SECP sandbox. The uncertainty most likely attaches to the earlier phase, or to the SBP-side payment flow, which is genuinely opaque.

Why this matters to us: it changes our positioning from “they are unlicensed and we are careful” to something stronger and true — we will walk the same ladder Oraan walked, sandbox → licence, but start guarantee-backed and CDC-custodied from day one. We have stopped saying the other thing.

Money Fellows' “7–8%” is not a default rate

The figure circulating publicly is widely read as their default rate. It is not. Money Fellows publishes no default rate at all. The 7–8% is empty-slot fill — positions the platform funds itself — financed by a US$2.25m Symbiotics debt facility. Their disclosed scale is real (~US$1.5bn processed, ~350k monthly actives, profitable in 2025); the loss number is simply not public.

The consequence is awkward and worth saying out loud: the promise we are making — that Halqa covers member losses — goes further than the benchmark actually does. We should be precise about that rather than claim parity.

3 · The direction — a committee is two different people

Every committee is two transactions wearing one coat. Reading them as one is why informal committees fail and why one-size-fits-all rules cannot fix them.

 The Saver — late turnThe Consumer — early turn
What they are doingSaving with discipline. Paying in for months, collecting at the end.Borrowing from the group. Receiving the lump sum before paying most of it in.
Risk to the groupNear zero. By the time they could walk away they have already paid in more than they will receive.Effectively all of it. Their forward liability is the whole exposure of the circle.
Their real complaintTheir money sat dead for a year while inflation ate it.Nothing — they got what they came for.
What we give themA return (Vault, CDC-held) and near-zero friction. CNIC, locality, job. No guarantee required.Verified income, banding to what they can service, a personal guarantee, and a pledged buffer or a secured asset.

Worked example. Ten members, Rs 10,000 a month, a Rs 100,000 pot. Turn 1 collects Rs 100,000 having paid Rs 10,000 — Rs 90,000 of forward liability. Turn 10 pays for nine months then collects; they cannot meaningfully default. Same circle, same rules, two completely different credit events.

The test we hold ourselves to — your warning, in our words.

Every control above is either invisible (position, pricing, banding) or opt-in (the products). A saver joining a circle still experiences an ordinary committee: pick a circle, pay each month, collect your turn. If a member ever has to understand the words “saver” or “consumer,” we have over-engineered it away from a real committee — which is the line you told us not to cross.

Prevention does roughly ninety percent of the work

Your mentor's doctrine — don't build enforcement, build a system where default is irrational — is the organising principle, not a slogan. In order of cost to us:

  1. Eligibility filtering. Members are only shown circles their verified means can service. Free, invisible, and it does a collections team's job before there is anything to collect.
  2. Cross-product collateral. Every member in a pledged Vault or an asset committee is pre-secured; a miss self-cures from their own units. We drive adoption with fee discounts and price honestly for everyone else.
  3. Position quarantine. New members take late turns only until two clean completed circles plus verification. New risk never starts at the front. Position is the collateral — and it costs nobody a rupee of deposit.
  4. Identity and reputation. Consented CNIC, home locality and public job; a reliability record built from real payment events.

5 · The asks, and the questions only you can answer

Ask 1 — a sandbox cohort, or a named path to one

This is the highest-value door in the whole plan and the only one we cannot open by paying someone or hiring someone. The precedent is exact: Egypt's central bank ran a dedicated ROSCA cohort, and that is what let Money Fellows exist and reach profitability. SECP's fourth cohort theme was Islamic micro- and nano-finance and “new takaful models” — essentially a description of us. We would pilot the guarantee model, capped and supervised, to replace our simulated default number with a real one before anyone's savings are at scale.

Ask 2 — two introductions

An AMC or CDC contact for the Vault, and a bank or PSP for collection. Both are commercial conversations rather than regulatory ones, which is exactly why a warm introduction is worth more than anything else you could hand us. Collection in particular is our binding constraint and sits outside SECP's remit entirely.

Ask 3 — your read on four lines

1 · The balance-sheet line. Where exactly does “Halqa covers defaults from its own funds” stop being an ordinary business cost and start requiring an NBFC or insurance licence? This is the single question that most changes what we build next.
2 · Reach inside the sandbox. Can we access an NCGCL-style guarantee and bureau/eCIB reporting within a sandbox, or only after an NBFC licence? The answer decides whether the pilot proves the real model or a stripped one.
3 · The float-backstop — the sensitive one. May savers' consented, return-paying Vault float part-fund the first-loss guarantee for consumers? We think this is capital-efficient and we think it is close to a line. We are raising it rather than quietly building it.
4 · Distributor or our own NBFC. Given where you want this to go, do you prefer we sit as a distributor to an existing AMC such as Mahaana, or build toward our own NBFC? We will sequence to your answer.

Two smaller ones if there is time: the CIS / lottery / derivative classification of the high-volume balloted committee with tradeable positions (we assume sandbox-only and will not ship it otherwise), and whether we read your margin-trading comment correctly.

6 · Hard questions, answered straight

The ones we expect, with the answers we will actually give.

“How many real members do you have?”

One. The platform is live on the internet and has been since 20 July, but we have not opened it to the public, because collection is not yet real and we will not take people's commitments on rails we cannot enforce. We would rather show you one honest user than a thousand we cannot collect from.

“Your default numbers are a simulation.”

Yes — a Pakistan-calibrated agent-based model, and we label it a simulation everywhere it appears. That is precisely the ask. A supervised pilot is how it stops being a model. We are not asking to be believed; we are asking to be measured.

“You say you cover defaults. With whose money?”

The fee spread, late fees, and a guarantee fee on protected circles — sized against an expected loss, provisioned the way a bank provisions. It is bounded, because early-turn exposure is itself bounded by eligibility banding and quarantine. The unexpected tail goes to a credit guarantee. If the pilot says the provision is wrong, we reprice rather than absorb it quietly.

“What stops you becoming the next Rs 420m Karachi case?”

There is no pot. Not a policy, not a control we could relax under pressure — there is simply no account into which member money accumulates. Vault savings sit with CDC in the member's own name. The fraud you are describing requires custody we do not have.

“What if Raast PISP slips?”

Then we run on the waterfall that works today — Raast RTP one-tap approval, card-on-file recurring for salaried consumers, wallet deduct via 1LINK. PISP is upside, not a dependency. We have architected so switching it on is a configuration change.

“Who is your lawyer?”

We do not have retained counsel yet — it is budgeted and it is the next thing we buy. Our undertaking and personal-guarantee texts are lawyer-unreviewed templates today, and we flag that in every document rather than let it pass. We would not scale on unreviewed instruments.

“Why hasn't a bank done this?”

Because the people in committees have no formal credit footprint to underwrite, and the ticket sizes are far below a bank's cost to serve. The committee already solved that with social collateral. We are not competing with the committee — we are giving it a record, a schedule, and a backstop.

7 · Our red lines, and our weaknesses

Lines we will not cross — stated before anyone asks us to

  • We will not hold the pot. If a design requires custody of member money, we do not build it — we find the licensed custodian who should hold it instead.
  • We will not surveil members or use out-of-court pressure. Deterrence stays legal and transparent: courts, guarantees, bureau reporting once permitted, and a platform record. Anything else is the behaviour of the fraudsters we are displacing, and it is unlawful under PECA and the data framework.
  • We will not launch the balloted, tradeable-position “Bazaar” committee outside a sandbox. We can see it looks like a collective investment scheme, a lottery and a securities market at once. It stays unbuilt until a regulator says the words.
  • We will not label a product Shariah-compliant until it has been reviewed. Products with a conventional early-access fee say so plainly in the consent text.
  • We will not report to a credit bureau until we are lawfully permitted to. We queue the records; we do not file them.
