1 · Summary and basis of preparation

A committee is a fixed arrangement between a group of people. Each member pays the same amount every period; each period the whole pot is paid to one member; after one rotation every member has paid the same total and received the same total. It requires no lender and no capital.

Approximately 37 per cent of Pakistani adults participate, some 52 million people, and an estimated Rs 4 trillion rotates through committees each year. Thirteen per cent of adults hold a bank account. The repayment history these arrangements generate exists in no written form.

Halqa records that history. Contributions move directly between members over Pakistan's domestic payment rails; Halqa observes each settlement and writes it to a double-entry ledger linked to national identity. Halqa does not take possession of member money at any stage, which is simultaneously the regulatory position, the principal fraud control, and the reason the product can be offered to members at no charge.

1.1 Four points of substance

The risk in a committee is a single quantity. It is the obligation carried by whoever has just collected, it is largest at the first seat, and it falls to zero at the last. Every eligibility rule and every price in this document is a function of that quantity rather than of a general assessment of members.

The prices are derived. The insurance premium is approximately 1.5 times the default rate, and that relationship holds independently of circle size, so the entire fee schedule rests on one input which the first hundred completed circles will measure.

Members pay nothing to join and nothing to contribute. Revenue arises from optional priced services and from counterparties. Two earlier Pakistani attempts charged members directly and neither reached scale.

2 · The instrument

2.1 Definition

A committee has four parameters: the number of members n; the contribution c paid by each member every period; the period itself; and the pot, which is n × c and is paid in full to one member each period. Over n periods every member pays n × c and collects n × c once. Total inflow equals total outflow, and the arrangement holds nothing at rest.

The only variable between members is the period in which they collect. A member collecting in the first period receives the pot having paid one contribution and owes the remainder across the cycle, which is an advance repaid in instalments at no charge. A member collecting in the final period has paid every contribution before receiving anything, which is saving under a commitment device. The same schedule delivers both, funded by the members themselves.

2.2 Forward liability

At the moment a member collects, they hold the pot and still owe every contribution that remains. That obligation is the forward liability.

L(k) = c ( n − k )     where k is the seat, counted from one

It is largest at the first seat, falls by exactly one contribution with each later seat, and is zero at the last. It is the only quantity in the arrangement genuinely at risk.

Exhibit 1 — Forward liability by seat. Twelve members, contribution Rs 10,000, pot Rs 120,000.

Risk in a committee is therefore not distributed across the members. It is concentrated in the early seats and decays linearly. The consequence is that the platform does not require a general system of member vetting; it requires a system that determines which member may occupy which seat. Sections 6, 11 and 14 are all applications of this.

3 · Architecture and regulatory position

3.1 The money path

When a member contributes, funds move from that member's account to the receiving member's account. Halqa initiates the instruction, receives confirmation that the transfer settled, and writes the settlement to the ledger. Halqa holds no balance belonging to a member and has no ability to spend member funds.

3.2 Consequences of the money path

Members fund one another directly, no balance is issued or held on their behalf, and payment instructions are initiated over a licensed aggregator's infrastructure under a merchant agreement. Second-stage savings are deployed by a licensed asset manager under that manager's own licence. The position is structural and does not rest on any undertaking Halqa gives.

The same decision is the principal fraud control. Every large committee fraud recorded in Pakistan required a central pool of members' money under one person's control. No such account exists here, and the protection cannot be weakened under commercial pressure because there is nothing to weaken.

Researchers at Information Technology University Lahore and the University of Washington, funded by the Gates Foundation and Karandaaz Pakistan, published the same architecture in 2018 on regulatory grounds, concluding that funds flowing through a committee application should be directed to an individual's own wallet with the application acting as a conduit. Verified against the published paper.

Collection is attempted on the member's payday rather than on the due date, since the account balance is at its monthly maximum at that point. 4.3 Netting the collector's contribution Build

On the round in which a member collects, that member's own contribution is deducted from the pot rather than collected from their account. The amount released is (n − 1) × c. The setting defaults to enabled and is not exposed as a choice.

A round's payout cannot release while any contribution in that round is unpaid, including the recipient's own. On the morning a member collects, their balance is at its lowest because the pot has not yet arrived; if their collection instruction fails, their own unpaid contribution blocks the payout that would have funded it. Netting removes that case. It does not remove the general rule, and a different member failing to contribute still blocks the round.

4.4 Raast

Raast is the State Bank of Pakistan's instant payment system. Its participants are regulated institutions: banks, microfinance banks, electronic money institutions and payment system operators. A financial technology company does not join directly. Halqa reaches it as a merchant of a licensed payment aggregator, which is a commercial contract with an entity already holding the licence and creates no partnership with a bank.

On Raast at present, automatic collection means a notification the member approves; it is not a silent debit. Silent debit exists only on wallet and card mandates. The payday mechanism operates as designed, since the request is issued on payday morning when the balance is at its maximum, but it requires an approval.

Outstanding question on the payment path

One question determines whether the money path in section 3.1 holds in operation: whether Halqa can initiate a Raast request-to-pay in which the payee is another member's Raast ID rather than Halqa's own merchant account.

If it can, funds move member to member, Halqa is never in the payment path, and the ledger requires no change, since it already models this. If it cannot, funds settle into the aggregator's account and Halqa instructs a payout to the recipient; the licensed aggregator holds the funds and Halqa holds an instruction right with no beneficial interest. That case requires a settlement-account entity in the ledger and a written legal opinion confirming it does not constitute custody by Halqa.

The question is to be put in writing to each candidate aggregator and to the operator of the domestic switch before any agreement is signed. It appears as the first item in the build sequence in section 16.

Implementation Gate 2

The payment layer in the codebase is an interface with a sandbox implementation behind it and a defined soft-failure path where no provider is configured. Raast becomes one further provider behind that interface, and nothing above the provider layer changes. The capabilities required are alias resolution returning account number and registered title; creation of a request-to-pay; status retrieval; a credit operation for payouts; and a signed callback endpoint.

  • Every request carries the internal payment-attempt identifier as its end-to-end reference. The callback settles against that reference inside the existing database transaction, so a duplicated callback has no effect. This pattern is already in use.
  • A daily settlement file from the aggregator is matched line by line against the ledger, with any mismatch raising an alert.
  • Expiry, decline and insufficient funds are distinct states driving different responses: reissue the request; escalate and offer a second rail; re-attempt on the next incoming-credit signal.
  • Rollout is aggregator sandbox, then a Rs 10 live transfer between two accounts under Halqa's control, then one live circle, then general availability.

Terms required in the merchant agreement

  1. The applicable tariff for each Raast scheme, and the position if the State Bank revises it.
  2. The third-party payee answer above, in writing.
  3. Same-day availability of funds to the recipient, the payout date being fixed.
  4. Callback delivery guarantees and replay behaviour.
  5. A per-transaction limit and a monthly volume commitment Halqa can meet.
  6. Refund and reversal mechanics, a reversed contribution after a payout has released being the most damaging state in the system.

The formation auction prices time and is not presented as Shariah-compliant. It is offered alongside the two free alternatives and is a single event at formation, distinct from the per-round discount auction excluded in section 2.3.

Verifiable ballot

  1. At formation the server generates a random seed and publishes only its hash, withholding the seed. The hash cannot be reversed and commits the server to that seed.
  2. During the confirmation window each member's device submits its own seed. Members do not see one another's.
  3. At the draw the server publishes its seed. Any member can hash it and confirm it matches the value published at formation, establishing that it was fixed before any member seed was known.
  4. Members are ordered by a keyed hash of the server seed combined with every member seed. All inputs appear in the receipt, so the ordering can be recomputed independently.

Neither the server nor any member can influence the outcome, the server being committed before it sees the member seeds and no member seeing another's.

5.5 The time value spectrum

A committee delivers an advance to the members who collect early and a savings return to the members who collect late, and the spectrum prices that difference. Where it is enabled, a member taking a seat in the first half pays a premium which is distributed to the members taking seats in the second half, in proportion to the number of rounds each of them waits. Where it is disabled, every seat costs the same and the order is a scheduling choice alone.

6.2 Display

The discovery screen shows only circles in which the member has both an eligible free seat and sufficient affordability headroom, so the eligibility system produces no refusals in ordinary use.

6.3 Tenure

A new account's opening score of 700 records nothing. Every new member, at any score, may claim only one of the last three seats of any circle, of any size and duration. The restriction is released after two completed circles with clean payment records and a manual verification step. Worst-case exposure to an unproven member is two contributions, being Rs 20,000 on a Rs 120,000 pot.

6.5 Circle authenticity, known circles Build

The organiser of a committee is the hub by design, it being the organiser's function to decide who is admitted, so a test of whether all members know one another would fail most genuine committees. The test applied is spoke integrity: every link between the host and a member confirmed independently from both ends. Attestations are private and non-repudiable. Open circles are not asked, strangers being the expected condition.

Device and network clustering between host and members is an automatic denial, and roster overlap across a single host's circles is monitored. A workplace circle failing the test may run as a standard circle, or use a verified employer as an institutional anchor.

7.2 Group vote Build

The vote applies to Level 3 in uninsured circles. It runs for 72 hours and is decided by a majority of votes cast, excluding the requesting member, with a quorum of 50 per cent of the roster. Where quorum is not reached the request escalates to Halqa for review rather than lapsing. The host votes as one member and breaks ties, and has no veto. The reason for a request may be submitted as a voice recording as well as text; approximately 31 per cent of the adult population can read a text message. Reported.

7.3 Restitution

Contributions return at the end of the cycle. Halqa holds no pool, so the amounts are owed by identified members.

A member departs after paying p contributions without having collected. The circle contracts from n seats to n − 1, so every remaining pot falls from n·c to (n − 1)c. Members who collected in rounds 1 to p each received n·c where they would otherwise have received (n − 1)c, and are ahead by c.

Each of those p members owes the departing member c, settled at completion. Total restitution is p × c, nominal.

The ledger invariant at close is that every continuing member's total received less total paid equals zero, and every departed member is returned to zero less any fine. The calculation is recursive, a second departure being computed against the adjusted roster.

No adjustment is made for the passage of time. A member leaving in the second month of a twelve-month circle loses one contribution and waits ten months for the remainder. The fine is divided 70 per cent to the remaining members, whose pots contracted, and 30 per cent to Halqa as administration.

7.4 Insured circles

Where cover is enabled the circle does not contract. The facility in section 13 takes the vacated seat, contributes each round, and collects that seat's pot at its scheduled turn. Every remaining pot stays at n·c and no member's payout changes. No restitution arises between members, the party that gained being the facility, which returns the departing member's contributions at close less any fine. No vote is required at Level 3, the group bearing no loss; uninsured circles retain the vote because the group does bear the loss.

7.7 Recovery

A recovery case records the outstanding amount, penalties and a rehabilitation fee of 10 per cent, linked to the member's identity number and timestamped.

Signing a personal guarantee and failing to pay is a civil matter in Pakistan and carries no imprisonment. The only route to criminal liability was the offence relating to dishonoured cheques, and cheques were removed from the model. Asset recovery proceeds by summary suit under Order XXXVII of the Code of Civil Procedure: the signed undertaking and the guarantee qualify as a written contract for a fixed sum, the defendant has ten days to seek leave to defend, and a decree is executable against assets. This requires a court decree and takes months. Verified.

The evidence pack is assembled on demand and comprises the signed undertaking, the mutual guarantee, identity linkage, the address pinned at signup, the timestamped payment history, the circle message log and the signature hashes. It is provided to counsel or to a court, and not to the circle.

A defaulting member's home address is not published to the circle. The relevant application-distribution policy prohibits the underlying data use even where the user has consented, and address exposure is the practice associated with the lending applications removed from distribution in Pakistan. The address is used for service of legal notice, for which purpose consent is taken at signup so that recovery does not stall on locating the member. The circle is shown status rather than address: recovery case open, notice served, score reduced by 200, platform access locked.

9.1 Derivation of the premium

Let q be the probability that a member defaults after collecting. Expected loss per member per cycle is q × c(n − 1)/2. Premium collected at rate r is r × c × n. Setting a three-times loading for adverse selection, operating cost and reinsurance:

r = 1.5 q (n − 1) / n   ≈   1.5 q

The rate is approximately 1.5 times the default rate and is independent of circle size.

10 · HYPER committees

A HYPER committee takes daily contributions from a fixed roster of four hundred members over a fixed cycle of sixty days, and pays six or seven members on each day. Positions are set once by auction, members have no visibility of one another, and the product is priced to withstand a default rate of thirty per cent.

10.1 Fixed structure

The roster and the cycle do not vary. Four hundred places are distributed across sixty days, being six recipients on twenty of the days and seven on the remaining forty, which allocates all four hundred exactly. The daily contribution determines the pot.

11 · Asset circles

In an asset circle the pot purchases a specified item rather than being paid in cash. The member takes possession at their turn; legal ownership is held by a third party until that member's obligation to the circle is discharged, and transfers on the close of the cycle.

11.1 Instrument

The structure is a conditional sale with retention of title. Possession and use pass at the member's turn; legal title does not, and is withheld until the condition is met. Under the Sale of Goods Act 1930 property in goods passes when the parties intend it to pass, and a seller may reserve the right of disposal until stated conditions are satisfied. Verified.

The retention period is the forward liability. For a member at seat k in a circle of n rounds, the amount owed at collection is L(k) = c(n − k), the retention period is n − k rounds, the discharge is that member's own remaining contributions, and title releases when the obligation reaches zero. At the final seat nothing is owed and nothing is withheld beyond the close formality in 11.4. The payment itself does not change.

11.2 Security trustee

The dealer is paid the full purchase price at the member's turn, out of the pot, and from that point has no financial interest in whether the member continues to pay. The party with the interest is the circle. Retained title is therefore assigned to a security trustee who holds it for the benefit of the circle's members, releases it when the member's obligation reaches zero, and enforces on default. The same institutional function is required for the savings products in section 12.

11.9 Leasing variant

A lease ending in ownership is available where a licensed leasing counterparty will write it, and requires that the counterparty rather than the pot finances the asset. The counterparty purchases and owns the asset and leases it to the member at a rental equal to the contribution; the member's pot is paid to the counterparty as an advance rental on arrival; the member's continuing contributions are paid to the counterparty as rentals, and the counterparty pays the contribution into the circle on the member's behalf. The counterparty carries the member's credit risk, secured by an asset it owns, and the circle is made whole irrespective of the member's conduct.

Title transfers at the end of the term under a separate unilateral undertaking, by gift or sale at a nominal price. A sale and a lease may not be combined in a single contract, so the arrangement requires three documents executed in sequence. The counterparty bears the risk of total loss, insures under takaful, and rental is suspended where the asset becomes unusable through no fault of the member. The retention structure ships first, requiring no counterparty to underwrite individual members.

11.10 Position

Halqa does not own the asset, hold title, finance the purchase, recover the asset or receive proceeds, and takes no credit risk beyond the published cover facility. Halqa introduces the member to the dealer and the trustee, schedules and collects contributions, records settlements, posts discharge records, files credit events and assembles the evidence pack.

Two matters require a written legal opinion before launch: whether introducing a member to a dealer and a trustee for a commission constitutes arranging credit as a regulated activity in Pakistan; and whether an assignment of reserved title to a trustee for the benefit of a group of individuals is enforceable as drafted.

14.5 Rewarded video

One advertising surface exists in the product. Advertising is not placed on payment screens, circle screens or in notifications.

A prize with a money value is a free-entry promotion rather than gambling only where there is no consideration to enter. Payment, purchase or a required action in exchange for entry engages the Prevention of Gambling Act and its provincial successors, and watching an advertisement is arguable consideration. Entry is therefore separated: a free daily entry requiring no cost and no advertisement may carry a cash prize; an advertisement-unlocked entry carries non-cash rewards only, being points, fee waivers and merchant vouchers.

A prize funded entirely by the platform as a gift, with no contribution from participants, is permissible under the Shariah view already applied to the existing prize mechanism in the codebase; a draw participants pay into is not.

Two forms are available for the reward itself. A chance mechanism, which carries the legal separation above and five operating conditions: it does not appear on a payment or circle screen; cash prizes credit against future contributions rather than into a wallet; there is no near-miss and no losing animation; odds and prize budget are published; and daily prize spend is capped. Alternatively, deterministic milestones at day 7, day 30, day 60 and circle completion, with the rewarded video unlocking the bonus tier, which produces the same advertising inventory and the same daily return loop without the chance element.

Rewarded video in Pakistan is reported at 1.50 to 4.00 US dollars per thousand completed views. At 100,000 daily active users with 30 per cent watching one video a day, monthly completed views are approximately 900,000, giving 1,350 to 3,600 dollars a month, or Rs 380,000 to 1,000,000. The surface cannot be scaled by adding placements.
