Executive summary

The problem

The committee is how Pakistan already saves — the country's third-largest savings channel, roughly Rs 4 trillion a year, used by about 37% of adults. It runs entirely on social trust, and it fails in two documented ways. The organizer absconds with the pool: Rs 420 million in the Karachi case, roughly US$2 million from 200 women in the Sidra Humaid case, and — from national survey data — 12% of committee users report having lost money to organizer or member fraud. Or the early collector stops paying, with no record, no recourse, and no return on the money while it waits.

The solution

Halqa records the committee rather than replacing it. Every payment becomes a timestamped, CNIC-linked entry on a double-entry ledger — admissible under the Electronic Transactions Ordinance 2002, and precisely what an informal committee can never produce. The first failure is removed structurally: there is no pool to steal, because Halqa never holds one. The second is removed arithmetically: turn position is the collateral, so an unproven member may take only late seats where the group's exposure to them is capped at roughly two installments.

Why no licence is needed

Pakistan's heavy licences each attach to a specific activity: EMI to holding e-money (Rs 200m capital), NBFC to lending or pooling funds, PSO/PSP to operating a payment system. Halqa performs none of the three. This is not a workaround — the regulated activities do not occur anywhere in the architecture. Notably, researchers at Information Technology University Lahore and the University of Washington, funded by the Gates Foundation and Karandaaz, independently proposed exactly this structure in 2018 as the correct legal design for a digital ROSCA in Pakistan (§9).

What shipped since the last report

Payday collection. The literature identifies regularity of income as the binding constraint on whether committees work at all. Halqa now collects on the member's salary day rather than the due date — while the balance is at its monthly peak — and verifies the declared payday from the platform's own collection outcomes, correcting itself when a member is wrong or dishonest. This is the single most important product decision the company has made, and it came from a chacha saying "I pay as soon as my pay arrives."

The plan

Gate 1 makes Halqa a company (~Rs 20–30k, two weeks). Gate 2 turns on real money via a Merchant Services Agreement with a licensed aggregator — a commercial contract, not a licence — plus NADRA Verisys, WhatsApp OTP and counsel's opinion. Total Rs 80,000–180,000 over 4–8 weeks, with the application live throughout. Nothing on the critical path is engineering. The second stage adds savings under CDC trusteeship (§8).

1 · What Halqa is, and why the structure is the product

Halqa is digital infrastructure for the Pakistani committee — a fixed group contributing an identical amount each round, one member collecting the whole pool each round, until everyone has collected exactly once. Halqa does not change this. It records it, schedules it, prices it, and converts payment history into a portable record of reliability.

Everything follows from one decision: Halqa never takes possession of member money. When a member pays, the money travels from their account directly to the recipient over Pakistan's own rails. Halqa observes the settlement and writes it to the ledger. That is the entire relationship between Halqa and the money — at every planned stage, including the second, where savings are held by a licensed trustee in the member's own name.

Why this is a legal position, not a slogan

  • EMI (SBP, Regulations for Electronic Money Institutions, 2019, Rs 200m capital) — required to issue or hold e-money. Halqa issues no balances and holds none.
  • NBFC for Investment Finance Services (SECP, NBFC Regulations 2008) — required to lend, or to pool and deploy funds. Members fund each other; second-stage savings are deployed only by a licensed AMC under its own licence.
  • PSO/PSP (SBP, Payment Systems and EFT Act 2007) — required to operate a payment system. Halqa operates none; it initiates over a licensed aggregator's.

The regulated activities do not occur anywhere in the system, so the licences that govern them cannot apply. The position was designed in from the first line of code, and it survives scrutiny because it is architectural rather than contractual.

The same decision is the primary fraud control. Every major committee fraud in Pakistan required one precondition: a central pool under one person's control. Halqa has no such account, and this protection cannot be weakened under commercial pressure because there is nothing to weaken.

Read the first two together: only 13% of adults have a bank account, and of those who manage to save, 33% use a committee while 63% hide cash in the house. The committee is not competing with banks. It is competing with a cupboard. Around 100 million Pakistanis have no access to formal financial services at all.

The correction — who committees actually serve

Two independent studies force a narrowing that most competitors do not make. Khan (2013), working in Dera Ghazi Khan, finds that regular income is a precondition for committee participation, that committees are therefore popular among the salaried, and that the genuinely poor are excluded as risky. Kamran's dissertation (2018) concurs that committees cannot substitute for formal financial institutions.

Kamran's 2017 field study, however, finds committees give the unbanked real control — and that none of his thirty informants had ever experienced a member default. Both are true once "the poor" is split in two:

The market, precisely defined

The destitute — irregular, insufficient income → excluded by the groups themselves

The regularly-earning unbanked — a tailor on Rs 17,000/mo, a driver on Rs 22,000 → served well, near-zero defaults

The committee filters on income regularity, not income level. It is a cash-flow-smoothing instrument for people with modest but predictable earnings and no bank — tens of millions of them. We state this openly because it is true, it is checkable, and it happens to be exactly the segment our payday engine is built for.

Two claims we have therefore retired: that Halqa serves "the poorest," and that committees substitute for formal finance. The stronger and defensible claim is that we are the on-ramp to formal finance — which is what the Sakh and the Credit Passport actually deliver.

3 · Mechanics — how a circle runs

StateWhat is true in itHow it ends
FORMINGMembers join by invite code up to the cap. Nothing is committed: anyone may leave, no money has moved. The minimum-to-start is a floor on pressing Start, not a lock on members.Start — host-initiated
ACTIVETurn order frozen, all rounds created with dates, round 1 open. Joining refused. Each round: members pay, host releases the payout, circle advances.Final round settles
COMPLETEDEveryone contributed identically and collected once. A clean completion raises the Sakh and counts toward unlocking earlier turns.—
CANCELLEDAvailable only from FORMING, because nothing is owed there.—

Start is one atomic write

Seat order is frozen, every round created, round 1's installments generated, and status flipped — all inside a single database transaction. A half-applied start would leave a circle in which turn position, the collateral of the entire model, could be disputed. The transaction budget is 25 seconds because this is the largest write in the system and it crosses a connection pooler.

Members choose their own seat, from a permitted set

At join, a member picks their turn. The permitted set is the intersection of their score band and their tenure. A request outside that set is refused with the reason. No one is silently moved, and a claimed seat is never taken away. If a circle starts under capacity, compaction sorts by band, then chosen seat, then join time — so the eligibility guarantee holds against the circle's actual size.

Chosen seats rather than platform ranking is deliberate: a reshuffle reads as the platform overriding the group, which is corrosive in a product built on pre-existing trust. The permitted set does the identical risk work, invisibly.

5 · Payday collection — the flagship

The academic literature identifies one variable as decisive for whether a committee works: regularity of income. Halqa now attacks that variable directly. This is the most consequential thing the product does, and it is live.

The mechanism

The evening before a member's declared payday, a balance-check notice goes out. On the payday morning — while the balance is at its monthly maximum, and before the installment is even due — the mandate executes. If the charge fails, a retry ladder runs through to the due date alongside the Raast one-tap request and WhatsApp nudge. Only if every attempt fails does the member meet the late-fee ladder at all. The due date stops being the collection event; payday becomes the collection event, and the due date becomes the deadline for failures.

Verification — the payday proves itself

Nobody in Pakistan can see inside a member's account: there is no open banking, and by standing directive no bank partner. So the declared payday is verified from evidence the platform legitimately owns, in four layers:

SignalHow it works
Account titleAt linking, the aggregator returns the registered holder name; it must match the member's CNIC name. Proves ownership.
Our own collection patternThe strongest signal, and it costs nothing. Every attempt is logged by calendar day. The first successful collection of each month marks a day money was provably present; two or more consistent months prove the payday. If a member declares the wrong day, the retry ladder finds the true one — the day retries begin succeeding is the payday.
Credit alertsOpt-in, on-device notification reading. Only aggregates leave the phone: a day, a coarse amount band, a source class. Two consistent months verify.
PayslipOne photo, downscaled on the device. Documentary verification on day one for those who have documents.

Consequences for a false declaration

When the evidence contradicts a declared payday mid-cycle, the verification is cleared, the salary fee discount stops (it now requires verified, not merely linked), a −15 credit event is recorded with its reason, and the member is told plainly why with instructions to correct it. Once per window, so nobody is punished twice for the same error.

Why this matters more than it sounds

Most committee default is passive — the money arrived, sat, and was spent before the due date. Payday collection deletes that entire category. What remains is active evasion: emptying the account, rerouting salary, revoking the mandate at the bank. Each of those is a deliberate act, visible and timestamped against a signed consent — which converts "I forgot" into documented bad faith with a court-usable evidence trail. That is as close to certain collection as Pakistan's rails currently permit, and it is closer than anyone else in the market gets.

The one piece still gated: execution against a live instrument. The decision logic, retry ladder and settlement path exist and pass their tests; the outbound authorisation waits on the Gate-2 merchant agreement. Wallet and card users are covered from day one; cardless bank accounts get the Raast one-tap until SBP's pull-payment scheme ships (in testing; realistically usable to us within 9–15 months).

6 · Default prevention

One formula organises the whole design. At the moment a member collects, their forward liability is what they still owe:

L(k) = c × (n − k)   c = contribution, n = members, k = turn position

Risk is not spread across the circle: it is largest at seat 1, falls by exactly one contribution per seat, and reaches zero at the last. A committee is therefore two credit products in one structure — early seats borrowing, late seats saving under a commitment device.

The seven controls

LayerWhat the system does
1 · IdentityCNIC photographed at signup, GPS home pinned, uniqueness enforced on CNIC/phone/email. Every obligation binds to a government identity.
2 · PositionBand and tenure define the claimable seats. Members below 550, and all new members regardless of score, may claim only the last three seats, never earlier than halfway. Graduation requires two clean circles and manual verification. Worst-case exposure to an unproven member is two contributions — Rs 20,000 on a Rs 120,000 pot.
3 · ScheduleFixed dates, escalating reminders, day-before balance check, and an internal grace window never shown in the interface: max(2, min(14, round(period × 0.23) − 2)).
4 · Price2% / 5% / 10% by lateness tier with matched score damage of −10 / −20 / −40. Post-payout default reaches −200.
5 · Payout controlsThree distinct mechanisms: a round's payout cannot release while any contribution in it is unpaid (which includes the recipient's own); a linked-account default withholds it; and where the forward-liability gate is on, a security shortfall is withheld from the payout itself, capped at 60% and released in steps as clean payments land.
6 · ConsequencePlatform-wide feature lock, public flag, recovery case for outstanding plus penalties plus a 10% rehabilitation fee. All CNIC-linked and timestamped; admissible under the ETO 2002.
7 · ContractWeekly e-signed undertaking (typed legal name plus drawn signature, stored with SHA-256 hash, IP and timestamp; clauses include liquidated demand for Order XXXVII recovery, acceleration, collection consent, bureau consent, arbitration) and an auto-executed mutual member-to-member guarantee — never to Halqa — required from every member before a circle can start.

Undertaking and guarantee texts remain internal drafts pending counsel review, a budgeted Gate-2 item.

7 · The credit system and the Sakh

The reliability score has a name now: Sakh — the Urdu word for exactly the thing it measures, economic credibility. It needs no explanation to a Pakistani user and cannot be borrowed by a foreign competitor.

Rebuildingbelow 550Last three seats, never earlier than halfwayMay not buy
Fair550–649Second half of the orderMay buy
Good650–749Any free seatMay buy
Excellent750+Any free seatMay buy

Scale 300–850, opening at 700. Hosting requires 700; the marketplace requires any non-Rebuilding band, i.e. 550. Tenure quarantine applies regardless of score, because a new account's 700 is an assumption rather than a record.

Why this signal is unusually strong

From Kamran's field interviews — a housemaid earning Rs 15,000/month

"I know that I have to pay the Committee instalment; it is necessary. We can compromise on rent or [utility] bills, but Committee [instalments] should not be missed."

Committee obligation ranks above rent and utilities in the household payment hierarchy — not because the committee can evict, but because failing the group is socially worse. This means committee repayment is not a weak proxy to treat cautiously; it is the member's highest-priority obligation, and therefore an unusually strong predictor of character. The Credit Passport is not exporting a soft signal. It is exporting the hardest one the household has.

Bureau interoperability — the strategic asset

TASDEEQ publishes a 200–600 scale; a linear invertible mapping onto our 300–850 is implemented, placing our 550/650/750 cutoffs at roughly TASDEEQ 382/455/527. Crucially, TASDEEQ already ingests data from non-financial contributors — utilities, telcos, insurers — so Halqa can join as an alternative-data contributor without a financial-institution licence and without a bank partner. Bureaus operate on reciprocity, so furnishing committee repayment data earns score reads in return.

The strategic value: informal committee repayment history exists in no written form anywhere. At scale, Halqa becomes the monopoly supplier of an entire data class the national credit system currently lacks — and the member gains formal credit from behaviour that was previously invisible. Contributor terms and the consent wording required by the Credit Bureaus Act 2015 are to be confirmed with TASDEEQ and counsel.

8 · The second stage — savings under trustee custody

The first stage records the committee. The second makes the money in it grow, without Halqa ever holding a rupee. No bank is involved at any stage of this plan.

The structure

Under the NBFC Regulations 2008, the assets of every regulated mutual fund in Pakistan are legally held by a trustee — in practice the Central Depository Company — while the asset-management company only manages them. The money path for a Halqa saver is: member → CDC collection account → fund units registered in the member's own name. Halqa never sits in that path. The model already operates in market: Pakistan's first digital-only AMC runs exactly this, CDC-custodied, with a Rs 1,000 minimum.

The licence it requires

An SECP mutual-fund distributor registration — a simplified registration permitting distribution of multiple AMCs' funds. Not an EMI, because Halqa holds no balances; not an NBFC, because Halqa deploys no funds. An investment-adviser licence is avoided by presenting an execution-only menu and recommending nothing. All three red lines stay uncrossed.

The products, and why they matter

ProductMechanism
Growth VaultIdle savings — typically a late-seat member waiting for their turn — routed into an Islamic money-market or income fund yielding roughly 10–11% against inflation of 11–12%. Committee money today earns zero while it waits, and Kamran's informants name this grievance in their own words: early turns are "happy," late turns "disappointed."
Safety VaultThe same holding, pledged, with a lien in favour of the circle. A missed installment settles from the member's own units through the standard settlement path. A member with a pledged buffer cannot default up to the size of that buffer — a Rs 25,000 pledge in a Rs 10,000 circle self-cures two and a half misses before the group is exposed.
The FloatContributions buy fund units in the payer's own name on pay-in and redeem on payout day. At 10–11% annual, one month of float is worth roughly 0.85–0.9% of the pool — about Rs 1,000 per round on a Rs 120,000 pool, accruing to savers rather than to nobody. Carries a liquidity buffer so no payout ever waits on a redemption.

How it strengthens the first stage: stage one prevents default with position and price; stage two adds property. Position, price, property — the three stack, and every member who adopts the vault reduces the exposure of every circle they sit in.

9 · The academic evidence

Four studies were read in full or near-full text for this report. Two findings deserve to be presented in any serious room.

Researchers independently derived our legal architecture

Mehmood, Razaq, Webster, Batool, Mustafa, Raza and Anderson (2018)

"Holding money deposits is not allowed without a license, and therefore the money flowing in and out of the ROSCA app could be directed to an individual's mobile wallet with the ROSCA app serving as a conduit."

Information Technology University Lahore with the University of Washington, funded by the Gates Foundation and Karandaaz Pakistan. That is Halqa's no-custody design, published eight years ago as the correct answer to the licensing constraint. We reached it from the fraud angle; they reached it from the regulatory angle; it is the same architecture.

They also mapped four models — and named why a notebook app cannot work

  1. Record-keeping app only — the organizer logs payments. The authors warn payment legitimacy "can be questioned and subject to fraud, limiting usefulness for credit rating."
  2. Social groups, payments through wallets — creates a real payment history usable for credit rating; default risk sits with members. This is Halqa exactly.
  3. Rating system connecting strangers — larger groups beyond one social network. This is our marketplace and public tier.
  4. Institutional ROSCAs — a bank carries default risk. Excluded by directive.

The commercial point: a digitised notebook cannot generate a credible credit history because the payments are asserted, not settled. Our ledger is a different category of asset from a competitor's tracker — not prettier, but recording a different kind of fact.

What else the literature establishes

  • The organizer is the guarantor of last resort. "If a group member failed to pay the instalments or disappeared after joining, then the organizer had to contribute the amount due from his/her own pocket." Selection risk and financial risk sit with the same person — cleaner incentive alignment than any bank's underwriting.
  • Locality is the fraud control. Members assess traceability, not character: "if he is local, then we know he lives here. His house and shop are here."
  • The committee is a shield against relatives, not only against oneself. "When the money is in someone else's possession, then people cannot ask for the money." Money at home is claimable by any relative in hardship; committed money is legitimately unavailable.
  • Privacy is a real anxiety. Members fear carrying the pot home and fear neighbours knowing it arrived. Digital settlement solves both incidentally — a benefit we would never have listed ourselves.
  • Design constraint: only 31% of committee users can send or receive a text message, and women participate at twice the rate of men while lagging in phone ownership. Literacy is a first-order design problem, not an accessibility afterthought.

10 · Engineering, security and verification

The decisions that cannot be retrofitted

Money is integer paisa in BigInt — no floating point anywhere, because binary floating point cannot represent most decimal fractions exactly and the residue is invisible until reconciliation fails. Rates apply as basis points with integer division. Every movement is a double-entry pair, so the ledger is self-checking. Every write carries an idempotency key, so a retried request — normal on mobile networks and under serverless timeouts — is applied once.

Topology: React and TypeScript on Vite; Node, Express and Prisma; PostgreSQL on Supabase reached through pgBouncer in transaction-pooling mode; API functions pinned to the same region as the database, which took the discovery path from roughly ten seconds to under one. Production migrations are additive-only hand-written SQL, so old and new code both stay correct during a deploy.

11 · The plan to operational

Halqa requires no financial licence while three conditions hold: no custody, no lending at scale, no bureau reporting. Each maps to one licence. Both stages are designed to keep all three intact.

Gate 1 — become a company (~Rs 20–30k, weeks 1–2)

SECP private limited incorporation via eZfile/LEAP (~Rs 3–5k self-filed, 2–5 working days) · FBR National Tax Number (free, 1–2 days) · provincial sales-tax registration · corporate operating account (1–2 weeks, in parallel) · trademark filing with IPO-Pakistan.

Rail economics — why the plan says Raast first

Rs 20,000 installment — card: 20,000 × 3.3% + 33 = Rs 693

Wallet: Rs 300–600 · intra-wallet or Raast: ≈ Rs 0

Members pay Halqa nothing, so every rupee of rail cost is unrecovered. A 12-member circle running one full cycle on card rails would burn Rs 52,272 in fees with no member revenue to absorb it. Raast at ≈0% is the only arithmetic under which free-for-members survives — and the chairman's ceiling stands: no member ever pays more than Rs 300 to make a payment at this tier. Card is therefore not offered on standard circles.

12 · Brand

The identity was rebuilt in August 2026 on a market read: the category's visual space is split between banks (corporate green and navy) and the 400 predatory loan apps that spent two years teaching Pakistan to distrust bright green, red urgency badges and countdown timers. The territory for calm, permanent and visibly halal was left vacant by the predators, and that is where Halqa now sits.

The mark — The Register

The poor's financial system does not run on an app or a bank. It runs on a notebook: a name in a column, a stroke against each month. So the mark is tally marks arranged in a ring — the organizer's register bent into the shape of the word halqa. Nine ivory strokes are the members who have paid; the tenth, gold and reaching inward, is whose turn it is now. It reads as a circle, a clock, a register and a sunburst at once, and it holds legibility down to 24 pixels because the eye catches the one stroke that breaks the pattern before it reads any detail.

Palette and voice

Deep Pine #07160F and Halqa Pine #123D30 ground everything; Live Gold #D9A93C marks the active turn and every call to action; Ivory #F6F1E4 replaces clinical white because dignity has warmth. Gold is retained as real brand equity but demoted from lead to accent — gold-led reads as greed and rhymes with the loan apps.

Copy leads in Roman Urdu with English beneath — "Apni committee, apnay log. Hisaab humara." This is not stylistic: only 31% of committee users can read a text message, and women, who participate at twice the rate of men, are least served by dense English screens.

Implemented in the live application: palette, mark, favicon and PWA theme. Concept marks, not final artwork; production requires a licensed display face.
